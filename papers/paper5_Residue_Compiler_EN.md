# FodaOS Unified Field Theory Series V: The Residueon Compiler — Bootstrap Fixed Points, the Residueon-Table Intermediate Representation, and Domain-Order Type Inference

**Abstract**. This paper gives the compiler-theoretic culmination of the FodaOS framework: it turns the five-operator computation of [Paper2] and the residueon-tower mathematics of [Paper4] into an algebraic compiler that is self-bootstrapping, verifiable, and capable of homogeneous multi-backend emission. The core results are organized in seven layers. (i) **Intermediate-representation layer** — the residueon table $(z,r,n)$ as the single universal IR; the Kleene bijection theorem (Theorem V1) turns compiler correctness from a test-coverage problem into a machine-decidable fixed-point problem. (ii) **Bootstrap layer** — the rigorous definition of self-bootstrapping (three constraints + bit-identical) and the three-tier theory (object level $C\cdot C=C$, meta level $\Psi^{(2)}$, evolution level), with a capping theorem ruling out a fourth order; this version adds **Theorem V36 (Banach contractive bootstrap)**: the measured Lipschitz constant of the bootstrap iteration is $L=\mathrm{poles}/\mathrm{tokens}\in[0.821,0.878]<1$, upgrading the bootstrap fixed point from pigeonhole existence (Theorem V3) to **existence, uniqueness, and exponential convergence** (fixed-point exponent $\alpha=0.234$ bit/iteration). (iii) **Trust layer** — the three-compiler triangular cross-check against Thompson's "Reflections on Trusting Trust" attack, plus the fourth-corner cross-language reader. (iv) **Pipeline layer** — the field-theoretic reconstruction of the four compilation stages (lexical/syntactic/semantic/code-generation) and the layer-collapse theorem, adding **Proposition V38 (priority-chain duality group)**: the seven-level precedence parsing chain carries a $(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3$ duality structure (logic/comparison/arithmetic, three mutually independent De Morgan–style flips). (v) **Type layer** — Domain-Order Inference (DOI): seven rules perform constraint solving over the **divisibility lattice** (composition takes $\mathrm{lcm}$ rather than unification; minimization takes the least fixed point), turning the lazy field-extension mathematics of [Paper4] Theorems 23/24 into a compile-time algorithm; shell polymorphism $\forall k.\tau@k$ is a new programming-language concept; the five-layer stepless tower (§14.6) automates domain selection into two-coordinate lookup plus lazy pool extension. (vi) **Optimization layer** — the Möbius conformal view of compilation (optimization passes preserve the cross-ratio invariant) and gear-shift geometry, adding **Proposition V37 (structural termination of finite-field equality saturation)**: the external-fuel dependence of equality saturation (the egg/ROVER/eqsat family) is replaced by the boundedness of the finite-field value space, and an information-theoretic Shannon upper bound is given for residual simplification (measured simplification rate 23.9% versus a source-coding bound of 23.6%); the optimization-pass spectrum (§16.5) is implemented in pure ΨLang in layers. (vii) **Physics layer** — the homogeneous multi-backend theorem and "the residueon table is the chip mask", adding **Proposition V39 (prime-pool–RNS structural correspondence)**: the prime pool and the modulus chain of RNS-CKKS homomorphic encryption are structurally isomorphic on the NTT-friendly prime admission criterion, together with the industrial-anchor check that 2025 photonic processors (Lightmatter/Lightelligence) have entered production AI workloads. Beneath the axiom layer, this paper introduces a **number-theoretic constant layer** (§2.4): every compile-time constant must carry an independently re-computable number-theoretic identity (Weyl group order / factorial unification, Coxeter-number arithmetic, Ramanujan–Nagell constants, the $6h{+}1$ prime family), and pushes the conformal view of compilation (Proposition V8) to its first **machine-verified finite-group anchor** $D_4=\langle C,I\rangle$ (generated by the Cayley transform and inversion, elliptic criterion $\mathrm{tr}^2/\det{=}2$, §15.4). All measurements are anchored by the dual-engine bit-identity criterion of the on-machine self-compilation tower (V11-043 through V11-048). This version simultaneously performs **thematic convergence**: seven results incongruent with the compiler main line (perfect-number image spectra, cognitive-matter phase transitions, vortex–residueon isomorphism, Paley geometric fingerprints, etc.) are removed after review, keeping the main text focused on compiler theory proper. The related-work comparison is extended to seven groups — CakeML (proof-producing bootstrap), the GNU Mes full-source bootstrap chain, the equality-saturation family, RNS homomorphic compilation, ZX-calculus, reversible programming languages, and photonic compilers (§23.8–§23.14).

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Problem Statement: The Triple Crisis of Compilers

The first four papers established the mathematical–physical–computational foundation of FodaOS: [Paper1] gives the physical program of the mother equation and the residue field; [Paper2] proves the Turing completeness of the five-operator system (⊙ ◦ ≋ ↡ ∆) and gives the WASM instruction layer; [Paper3] completes the exactification of quantum and combinatorics over GF(65537); [Paper4] establishes the theorem system of the residueon tower (Theorems 1–29: trichotomy, reciprocity siting, dihedral spectrum, the mathematical layer of domain-order inference). Yet a mathematically complete computational framework that cannot **compile itself** is a program, not a system. The compiler is the sole channel by which this framework moves from mathematics to engineering, and the classical compiler-technology stack faces three structural crises:

**Crisis One: the correctness crisis**. The correctness of industrial compilers (GCC, LLVM) rests on test coverage and fuzzing; semantic equivalence has no machine-decidable criterion. Verified compilers (CompCert) prove correctness at enormous engineering cost, but every added language feature must be re-proved, and the proof itself trusts the underlying axioms. The root cause: classical IRs (AST/SSA/bytecode) are **syntactic objects**, and deciding whether two syntactic objects are semantically equivalent reduces to axiomatic semantics — it cannot be closed at the IR level.

**Crisis Two: the trust crisis**. Thompson, in *Reflections on Trusting Trust* (1984), showed that a malicious compiler can plant a backdoor while compiling itself, and that the backdoor **exists in no source file** — auditing the source can never find it. This is a structural vulnerability of self-referential systems: the trustworthiness of a compiler cannot be proven by the compiler itself. The countermeasures (Diverse Double-Compiling, etc.) demand two independent compilers cross-validating, but "independence" is hard to achieve in the classical ecosystem — the major compilers share front-end ideas, standard libraries, and even developer communities.

**Crisis Three: the homogeneity crisis**. A language targeting $N$ backends (x86/WASM/GPU/ASIC/quantum) must maintain $N$ code generators and $N$ semantic interpreters. Cross-backend behavioral drift (floating-point rounding, memory ordering, saturation semantics) is a chronic pain of distributed verification and reproducible science. The root cause: each backend defines "the physical meaning of operands" on its own, lacking a backend-independent semantic contract layer.

**The algebraic opportunity**. The first four papers prepare algebraic answers to all three crises: (a) [Paper2]'s five operators are a backend-independent semantic contract — the operator algebra is precisely defined over GF(p), decoupled from hardware implementation; (b) [Paper4] Theorems 23/24 (lazy field extension, k-depth hierarchy) mathematize "types" as "domain orders"; (c) the residueon $(z,r,n)$ triple is both an algebraic atom and a syntactic carrier ([Paper4] §3.5 already verifies the lossless transcription from syntax tree to residueon table). Consequently, the compiler need no longer found its mathematics on syntax transformation, but on the **algebraic fixed point**. This is the point of departure of the present paper.

**Paradigm positioning: the third-generation compiler**. Placing the above crises and answers in the generational table of compilation technology, the position of this paper is clear:

| Generation | Representative | Mathematical basis | Correctness | Trust |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First (syntactic compilation, 1950s–) | GCC/LLVM | grammars & graph rewriting | test coverage + fuzzing | ecosystem audit |
| Second (proved compilation, 2000s–) | CompCert | operational semantics + proof assistants | proof obligations (re-prove per feature) | axioms + proof kernel |
| **Third (algebraic compilation, this paper)** | the residueon compiler | GF(p) algebra + fixed points | **round-trip fixed-point criterion (machine-decidable)** | **triangular cross-check + number-theoretic constant identities** |

The watershed among the three generations lies not in engineering volume but in the **mathematical form of correctness**: the first generation delegates correctness to tests (empirical), the second to proofs (obligation), the third **compresses it into an algebraic property of the data structure itself** (the Kleene bijection of the residueon table) — correctness is no longer an assertion about the compiler but an intrinsic property of the IR. The constant layer (§2.4) pushes the same algebraization to the most inconspicuous corner: even magic numbers are replaced by number-theoretic identities. This is the precise meaning of "paradigm revolution" in this paper: **not a better compiler, but a migration of the very concept of correctness**.

### 1.2 Main Results

The theorems and propositions of this paper are numbered with the V prefix (distinct from [Paper4]'s Theorems 1–29). Main results:

1. **Theorem V1 (Kleene bijection)**: post-order emission and stack reconstruction are mutually inverse if and only if the residueon table is an isomorphic image of the syntax tree (the $n$ field encodes tree structure). Round-trip consistency $f(T)=T$ thus becomes a **machine-executable correctness criterion** for the compiler, compressing the correctness argument from "test coverage" to "a single round-trip comparison".
2. **Theorem V2 (rigorous definition of self-bootstrapping)**: self-bootstrapping = a compiler $C$ takes its own residueon table as input and outputs a residueon table bit-identical to itself. The three constraints (input = itself / output bit-identical / the compiler understands residueon syntax) turn "self-bootstrapping" from a slogan into a decidable predicate; two classes of pseudo-bootstrap (candidate body, linear reduction) are thereby excluded.
3. **Theorem V3 (existence of the finite-field fixed point)**: a compiler endomorphism over GF$(p)$ has a Kleene fixed point; by the finiteness of the finite field, iteration must enter a cycle within $\le p^{\dim}$ steps, and bit-identical convergence is the trivialization of the cycle. This guarantees that bootstrap-tower iteration is mechanically executable and necessarily terminates (at the decision level).
4. **Proposition V4 (three tiers and the cap)**: self-bootstrapping is stratified into the object level ($C\cdot C=C$, instruction closure), the meta level ($\Psi^{(2)}\cdot\Psi^{(2)}=\Psi^{(2)}$ with $\pi(\Psi^{(2)})=C$), and the evolution level (the Darwin engine); the third tier has no independent Kleene fixed point, and there is no fourth-order bootstrap (the Gödel boundary).
5. **Proposition V5 (trust upper bound of triangular verification)**: three independent-paradigm compilers (the Rust verification engine / the ΨLang self-implementation / residueon-native) produce the same bit stream on the same input, so the posterior probability of a hidden defect in any single implementation is multiplicatively suppressed by independence; this is a structural defense against the Thompson attack (the attack must infect three code-disjoint paradigms simultaneously).
6. **Theorem V6 (layer collapse)**: QIR (quantum intermediate representation) and WASM opcodes are different labels of the same object in the residue field; the intermediate layers of the compilation stack (token→AST→IR→bytecode→machine code) are **scaffold projections** of the same residue field from the algebraic viewpoint, collapsible in principle to a single conformal mapping [PROPOSAL].
7. **Theorem V7 (Domain-Order Inference, DOI)**: seven Hindley–Milner-style inference rules (Var/Const/Op/Root/Embed/Project/Auto) implement the lazy field-extension strategy of [Paper4] Theorem 23; the irreducibility criterion of the (Root) rule, $a^{(q-1)/d}\neq1$, needs only one modular exponentiation and completes at compile time; domain-order inference is structurally isomorphic to HM type inference, with type inference as the $k=0$ special case. Shell polymorphism $\forall k.\tau@k$ is a new programming-language concept.
8. **Proposition V8 (Möbius conformal view of compilation)**: compiler optimization passes can be regarded as elements of the Möbius group, with semantic invariants corresponding to cross-ratios; verifying the compiler = verifying that the group action preserves invariants [PROPOSAL, equivalence unproved].
9. **Proposition V9 (multi-backend homogeneity)**: a unique operator layer with diverse hardware implementations is a sufficient condition for cross-backend bit-identity; the residueon table's $(z,r,n)$ maps directly onto hardware parameters (PE instance/input/fan-out), "the residueon table is the chip mask" [PROPOSAL].
10. **Measurement anchor**: the current height of the self-compilation tower — V11-043 (12-keyword self-compiler, 166 poles), V11-047 (+string interpolation, 342), V11-048 (+triple-quoted multi-line strings, 420), V11-046 (compile-execute closure $r=70$). The bit-identity check of each rung takes the Rust verification engine as the reference, a record retained from before that engine's retirement.
11. **Proposition V15 (lossless packing of the hardware frame and its exact inverse)**: an **encoding-level bijection** between the residueon table and the hardware frame — $(z{\ll}26)\mid(r{\ll}8)\mid n$ ($z{:}18\,|\,r{:}18\,|\,n{:}8$, 44 bit), with decoding as its exact inverse. This advances Proposition V9 ("residueon table as chip mask") from [PROPOSAL] to **encoding-level verified** (the physics level remains unverified), and extends the Kleene bijection of Theorem V1 to the full reversible chain AST ↔ $\mathcal T$ ↔ frame (§19.1).
12. **Proposition V5′ (the fourth corner: a cross-language independent reader)**: in response to the self-audit that "the independence of triangular verification is the soft spot" (Proposition V5), a cross-language serialization protocol for the residueon-table text plus a **zero-dependency Python independent reader** (zero code sharing) is introduced as the fourth corner. The first three corners verify **computational consistency**; the fourth verifies **representational consistency** — the layer where the Thompson attack actually lives (the backdoor is not in the source but in the interpretation of the artifact). The trust stack is thus raised to four levels: operators are defined by mathematics, constants by number theory, structure by fixed points, and **formats by an independently implementable ASCII protocol** (§8.5).
13. **Strengthened V1′ (deep fixed point)**: the single round-trip criterion of Theorem V1 is strengthened to the iterative criterion $F^N(T)=T\ \wedge\ \mathrm{fp}(F^N(T))=\mathrm{fp}(T)$ ($N=20$ measured, golden fingerprints 2722/541 fixed). The single-step criterion misses "accumulated drift"; the iterative criterion necessarily amplifies it; the fingerprint fixed point reduces continuous monitoring to a single integer comparison (§3.2).
14. **Orthogonality of the two lines of defense**: the Kleene bijection (structural layer, catching the three defect classes of $n$ truncation/overflow/reuse) and dual-engine bit-identity (semantic layer, catching shift asymmetry / missing node count / **domain-semantics leakage** — defects 4–6) are **orthogonal and both necessary**: defects 4–6 all pass the structural criterion, while the three defect classes collapse at the structural layer. A broken bit-identity precisely localizes the representational difference of the two implementations (Proposition V14, §9.2).
15. **The first runnable evidence of Theorem V6 (the legacy syntax bridge)**: two **syntactically completely different** source languages (brace syntax vs canonical indentation syntax) map onto the **same IR** with zero change to the residueon-table format and the Kleene bijection preserved. This lifts the layer-collapse theorem from pure PROPOSAL to "**partial verification**" (syntactic layer collapsed, backend layer pending) (§11.2).
16. **Negative result (channel-scoped fixed-point)**: fixed-point representation is **undefined within the GF channel** — `3*65536` equals $65534\neq196608$ in GF, and the finite field cannot be ordered, so comparison and rounding are undefined; but it is **architecturally feasible in the ℤ channel and the Q64 domain**: `<<`, `/`, `%`, and comparison are integer operators not reduced through GF, and decimal literals already enter computation directly (literals and addition/subtraction verified faithful; multiply/divide rescaling pending, O40). This delimits the cost boundary of the axiom-layer choice: **with the six operators as the sole arithmetic primitives of the GF channel, the cost is that fixed-point techniques cannot be used in that channel and must migrate to ordered channels** — the cost is migration cost, not loss of capability.
17. **Proposition V17 (reversible Futamura first projection, §8.6)**: the idempotence $F^2=F$ of Theorem V1 is precisely the mathematical meaning of "projection" in the phrase "Futamura projection" — the residueon table is the interpreter specialized to the source program, and it is **reversible**, whereas classical partial evaluation is not.
18. **Proposition V18 (binding-time monad, §8.6.1)**: the two constructors of the compile-time evaluator are exactly the binding-time annotations of partial evaluation (present = static, absent = dynamic), and the Kleisli composition of the Maybe monad is the divides/joins rule of BTA.
19. **Theorem N1 (k=5 fingerprint reachability, §21.1)** [VERIFIED]: the mirror fingerprint $\{4\}$ predicted by [Paper4] Theorem 26 has an execution path fully determined by **domain reachability**: the Fermat domain $p=2^{2^n}+1$ never contains $\zeta_5$ ($5\nmid p-1$), the Mersenne domain makes $\{4\}$ structurally unreachable ($v_2(p-1)=1$); GF(41) is the **smallest reachable domain** of the k=5 tower ($40=2^3\cdot5$ simultaneously supplies $\chi_5$, the $\{4\}$ fingerprint, and self-complement symmetry). This gives the (Root) rule of §12 its **first fifth-order live example**.
20. **Theorem V36 (Banach contractive bootstrap, §7.3)** [VERIFIED]: the Lipschitz constant of bootstrap iteration is measured at $L=\mathrm{poles}/\mathrm{tokens}\in[0.821,0.878]$ (six simplified versions all $<1$), so the bootstrap map is contractive in this scale metric — Banach's fixed-point theorem gives **existence + uniqueness + exponential convergence** (convergence exponent $\alpha=-\log_2L=0.234$), forming with Theorem V3's pigeonhole existence a two-layer structure of "algebraic existence × analytic uniqueness". The bootstrap artifact is simultaneously a **Kolmogorov-complexity fixed point**: $K(\mathrm{IR})=K(\mathrm{source})+O(1)$.
21. **Proposition V37 (structural termination of finite-field equality saturation, §16.4)** [structural observation]: equality saturation is introduced into the residueon-table optimization layer — classical implementations (egg/ROVER/eqsat) rely on external fuel or iteration caps for termination; in this framework, with a fixed table-length upper bound $m$, the equivalence-class space is finite ($p^{3m}$), so saturation is structurally bounded; combined with the cross-ratio guard (§15.1), it forms an optimization loop of "non-destructive rewriting + invariant checking". The gain of residual simplification is simultaneously bounded by Shannon source coding: measured simplification rate 23.9% versus a theoretical bound of 23.6% (Corollary V31.1).
22. **Proposition V38 (priority-chain duality group, §14.5)** [VERIFIED]: the seven-level precedence parsing chain (or / and / equality / comparison / add / multiply / operand) carries a $(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3$ duality group — the logic-level flip ($\|\leftrightarrow\&\&$, De Morgan duality), the comparison-level flip ($=\leftrightarrow\neq$), and the arithmetic-level flip ($+\leftrightarrow\times$) are mutually independent, each preserving parser structure while swapping only opcode dispatch; 8 duality variants in total. This is the first machine-verifiable group-theoretic characterization of parser-structure symmetry.
23. **Proposition V39 (prime-pool–RNS structural correspondence, §18.4)** [correspondence · VERIFIED structural layer]: the prime pool of this framework (NTT-friendly admission: $v_2(p-1)$ sufficiently large) and the standard modulus chain (prime chain / modulus chain) of RNS-CKKS/BFV homomorphic encryption share the same number-theoretic admission criterion — both are architectures of "NTT-friendly primes + CRT reconstruction". The difference lies in the target: HE uses the chain to control multiplicative depth (rescaling lowers the modulus), this framework uses the pool to control domain order and value range (mosaic single-domain execution). This gives a structural path for "executing the residueon table on homomorphic ciphertext" (unimplemented, O35).
24. **Proposition V20 (radical-ring correction, §21.2)** [VERIFIED]: $a^{1/n}$ belongs to the **exponent ring** $\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)$, not the field GF(p) — the old executor using the field inverse $n^{-1}\bmod p$ is in the **wrong ring** (example: in GF(65537), $8^{1/3}$ used to yield 4, but $4^3=64\neq8$; the correct $3^{-1}\bmod65536=43691$ gives $8^{43691}=2$, $2^3=8$ ✓). **Closure/split duality**: $p-1=2^{16}$ (Fermat poles) closes all odd radicals; $p-1$ containing odd factors (Mersenne type) enables splitting. This is the fundamental correction of the DOI (Root) rule from "field inverse" to "ring inverse".
25. **Proposition V21 (dual-path architecture V12, §21.3)** [VERIFIED]: operator = bytecode = IR, **three in one** — every QBC operator is a WASM opcode (downward compatible) and can collapse directly to x86-64, **skipping the WASM intermediate** (2 steps vs V11.5's 4). This is the **first engineering instance** of Theorem V6 (layer collapse): the WASM layer is folded away on the operator-direct path.
26. **Proposition V22 (self-describing domain marker, §21.4)** [VERIFIED]: the domain marker $z{=}31,\ r{=}p$ (the prime itself) makes the residueon table **self-describing** about its execution domain — after a serialize–deserialize round trip, the domain annotation and the poles are mutually inferable, and the Kleene bijection is preserved. The prime pool $\{65537,131071,786433,1376257\}$ can all serve as execution channels (total dynamic range $\prod p_i=9\,297\,248\,521\,260\,198\,002\,687>2^{72}$). This is the extension of Proposition V9 (multi-backend homogeneity) to the **domain layer**.
27. **Industrial-anchor check of the photonic backend (§19 supplement three)**: in 2025 photonic computing moves from vision to production — Lightmatter publishes in Nature a general photonic AI accelerator (running production ResNet/BERT/Atari-DRL workloads), Lightelligence's PACE integrates a 64×64 photonic array, and dedicated compilers for photonic-electronic hybrid systems (LightCode, 2025) begin to appear. This updates the physical background of §19's "residueon table as mask" from pure foresight to **foresight with an industrial reference** (this framework's direct-transcription route still has no real-machine verification; the boundary is unchanged).

### 1.3 Contributions Discovered for the First Time in This Paper

1. Turning compiler correctness from a test problem into a **fixed-point problem** (Theorem V1: the Kleene bijection criterion), with an executable round-trip bijection check;
2. A **decidable rigorous definition** of self-bootstrapping (Theorem V2, three constraints), distinguishing language self-hosting (textual closure) from physical self-bootstrapping (algebraic fixed point), and giving two exclusion criteria for pseudo-bootstrap;
3. **Compilation as fixed point** via stage encoding: the five compilation stages (LEX/PARSE/CODEGEN/OPTIMIZE/EMIT) are each encoded as one pole; the compilation process = field evolution, compilation completion = reaching the fixed point;
4. The **structural defense** argument of triangular cross-check against Trusting Trust (Proposition V5), together with the applicability boundary of its independence assumption;
5. The **domain-order-inference type system** (Theorem V7): for the first time taking the field-extension degree $k$ as the dimension of a type system, proposing shell polymorphism $\forall k.\tau@k$;
6. The gear-shift cost model and the compilation-quality criterion $Q$ (§17.3), turning "optimization quality" from empirical judgment into a computable objective;
7. The unification of the residueon table's triple semantics (IR node / physical pole / hardware parameter) and the hardware direct-transcription route of "the residueon table is the chip mask" (Proposition V9).
8. **The dual role assignment of the fields** (§3.1 note): V3 ($z$ as opcode, $n$ storing tree structure) and ΨLang2 ($n$ as opcode, a 127-instruction stack machine, $z$ storing semantics) choose **mutually dual** role assignments on the same triple format — thereby arguing that **the residueon table is a cross-implementation protocol**: format stability is non-negotiable, field semantics is a negotiable representational layer. Its corollary is that the location of the capacity bottleneck **migrates with the assignment** ([Paper4] Corollary 22.1).
9. **The encoding-level bijection of the hardware frame** (Proposition V15, §19.1): extending Theorem V1's Kleene bijection from AST ↔ residueon table to residueon table ↔ hardware frame, making the entire chain reversible everywhere — "residueon table as mask" thereby gains an encoding-level computable isomorphism (previously a pure design specification).
10. **The fourth corner and representational consistency** (Proposition V5′, §8.5): identifying the soft spot of Proposition V5 as "endogenous triangle ⟹ correlated failure", and supplementing **representational-layer** verification with a cross-language independent reader of **zero code sharing** — the Thompson attack lies precisely in "computationally consistent but representation tampered", and the fourth corner is so far the only means reaching that layer.
11. **Negative-encoding strings** (Proposition V16, D.9): using the value domain's **negative space** for references (string $=-(slot+1)$), repairing the largest known limitation of "strings degenerate to hashes" with **zero added syntax and zero IR-format change**; and giving its bit-identity trap (allocation sequences must be strictly mirrored; measured handles $-14$ vs $-7$) — the second instance of Proposition V14.
12. **The division of labor of the two lines of defense** (§9.2): the structural criterion (Kleene bijection) and the semantic criterion (dual-engine bit-identity) are **orthogonal and both necessary**, and it records for the first time the new defect class of **domain-semantics leakage** (`0-x` in GF is not negation but complementation to the modulus ⟹ a condition is perpetually true ⟹ infinite recursion), of the same family as [Paper4] Corollary 22.5's context-sensitive semantics.
13. **Implementation-level determinism guarantees** (D.12): discovering that non-nondeterminism can bypass the semantic layer's "random interface always returns zero" and leak from the **data structure** (the iteration order of a hash set); the fix is sorting before iteration, with a lint rule (O16). It argues that the "three no's" principle is not a slogan but a mechanically checkable constraint, and that it continues to yield dividends at the implementation layer (no-float / no-parallel / no-pointer-hash already exclude three of the four non-nondeterminism sources).
14. **The cost boundary of the axiom choice** (§19.3, D.13): two records pointing in opposite directions — (a) with the six operators as the sole arithmetic primitives of the GF channel, fixed-point representation is **undefined in that channel** (a negative result, rooted in the non-orderability of finite fields) and can only migrate to the ℤ channel and the Q64 domain, the cost being migration cost rather than loss of capability; (b) the same choice lets roughly 200 algorithms across cryptography/coding/signal processing/machine learning land losslessly on GF$(65537)$ (a positive result, D.13). **Axioms are not free**: this paper records both sides of the bill item by item.
15. **Proposition V17 (reversible Futamura first projection, §8.6)**: the idempotence $F^2=F$ of Theorem V1 is exactly the mathematical meaning of "projection" in "Futamura **projection**" — the residueon table is the interpreter specialized to the source program, and this specialization is **reversible**, unlike classical partial evaluation. ### 2.1 The Computational Base

**Field**. All arithmetic is carried out in GF$(p)$ with $p=65537=2^{16}+1$ (a Fermat prime, the interference/NTT layer); the probability/phase layer uses the Mersenne field GF$(p_2)$ with $p_2=131071=2^{17}-1$; cross-layer composition uses the CRT composite field ([Paper4] Axiom G). Zero floating point, zero rounding, zero platform variance — this removes the homogeneity crisis at its root: **when all values are finite-field elements, no floating-point semantic drift exists**.

**Operators**. The six operators ⊕ ⊙ ◦ ≋ ↡ ∆ constitute the semantic-contract layer, in order:

| Operator | Mnemonic | Arity | Semantics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ⊕ | GFADD | binary | field addition: $(a+b)\bmod p$ |
| ⊙ | GFMUL | binary | field multiplication: $(a\times b)\bmod p$ |
| ◦ | CLIFFORD | binary | the Clifford product: the geometric product of $\mathrm{Cl}(4,1)$ |
| ≋ | NTT | unary | Bragg/NTT resonance; identity on scalars, dispatched through calls on arrays |

The latter five operators (⊙ ◦ ≋ ↡ ∆) are exactly the five operators defined in [Paper2] §2.1–§2.5 and proven Turing-complete; ⊕ (field addition) stands first in the sequence and corresponds to GFADD in the opcode-mapping table of §21.3. **The relation between five and six is not "one more operator", but "completing the ring structure"**: ⊙ alone yields only the multiplicative semigroup; the introduction of ⊕ closes the operator set over the **field (ring) structure** of GF$(p)$ — [Paper2]'s Turing-completeness proof needs only the multiplicative side, while this paper's ALU and hardware mapping (§19, §21.3) need the full field arithmetic; this is the source of the two notational differences.

Key positioning (continuing [Paper2] §2.6): **the operator layer is the instruction set of the ALU, but the ALU does not bootstrap itself — operators are the axiom layer**. The essential difference from classical compilers: a classical compiler's "axioms" are machine-instruction semantics (defined by hardware-vendor documentation), whereas this framework's axioms are algebraic operators (defined by mathematics), depending on no vendor.

## 3 The Residueon Table as the Universal Intermediate Representation

### 3.1 Triple Semantics

The claim of the residueon table as the single IR (the single-IR principle, §3.3) rests on the **consistent carrying** of its fields across layers:

| Layer | $z$ | $r$ | $n$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IR (compiler view) | node type code (0=constant, 1=add, 2=multiply, 3=variable, …) | data payload (constant value / variable binding) | child count (leaf=0, binary=2) |
| Physical (field-theory view) | pole position $z_k$ | residue $r_k$ | pole order |
| Hardware (backend view) | PE type (adder/multiplier/Clifford unit) | PE input | FIFO fan-out |

The triple semantics is not a metaphor: the IR layer's node type code **is** the hardware layer's PE-type selector, and the $n$ field **is** the dataflow fan-out. A $(z,r,n)$ migrates from syntax-tree node to physical pole to hardware parameter with **no format conversion** — this is the data-structure premise of the "compilation = algebraic identity transform" route.

**Note (dual role assignment of the fields — the first evidence of a cross-implementation protocol)** [VERIFIED]. The triple-semantics table gives $z$ and $n$ their **canonical roles** ($z$ = type code, $n$ = child count). But the role assignment is not unique: two independent implementations choose **mutually dual** assignments, yet the two inter-operate perfectly at the residueon-table format level:

| Implementation | Role of $z$ | Role of $n$ | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **V3 (scientific verification)** | node type code **= ALU opcode** (22 + V13 extensions, including $z{=}29$ Return, $z{=}31$ DomScope) | child count (the faithfulness of $n$ is the bijection condition of Theorem V1) | $z$ is the **instruction**, the table feeds a stack machine directly; $n$ keeps tree structure for round-trip reconstruction |
| **ΨLang2 (engineering implementation)** | node type code (carrying syntactic/type semantics) | **127 opcode stack-machine instructions** ($n$:u8, instruction codes 0…126) | $n$ is the **instruction**, an entire stack machine packed into one u8 field; $z$ keeps the semantic annotation |

*Reading*. These are not two incompatible formats but **two encoding conventions over the same triple** — the triple itself is the protocol, and field semantics is agreed by the consumer. Two structural implications:

1. **Field semantics is a negotiable representation layer; format is a non-negotiable protocol layer.** Whether $z$ or $n$ is the opcode depends on the execution model (a stack machine needs a small field for instructions; tree rewriting needs $n$ faithful). The residueon table's qualification as a **cross-implementation protocol** comes from format stability (triple + post-order + faithfulness), not from fixed field roles.
2. **The location of the capacity constraint migrates accordingly** ([Paper4] Corollary 22.1's "insufficient field capacity"): under the V3 scheme $n$ carries tree structure and is thus constrained by $n\le255$ child count (the three defect classes of §9.2); under the ΨLang2 scheme $n$ carries opcodes and is constrained by opcode space $\le256$ (127 already half used), while $z$, decoupled from tree structure, has a different constraint form. **The capacity bottleneck of the same table appears in different fields** — explaining why the two implementations hit two different sets of engineering pitfalls in practice.

*Boundary*. The two implementations currently perform **no artifact inter-conversion** (no V3↔ΨLang2 residueon-table converter). Format isomorphism is a structural judgment; interoperability is not yet measured; listed as open problem O12.

### 3.2 Theorem V1: the Kleene Bijection

**Theorem V1 (Kleene bijection)** [VERIFIED]. Let $\mathcal A$ be the syntax-tree space and $\mathcal T$ the residueon-table space. The post-order emission function $\mathrm{emit}:\mathcal A\to\mathcal T$ (each node transcribed post-order into one row $(z,r,n)$) and the stack-reconstruction function $\mathrm{rebuild}:\mathcal T\to\mathcal A\cup\{\bot\}$ (leaves push $r$; an $n$-ary row pops $n$ items and assembles them by $z$) satisfy
$$\mathrm{rebuild}\circ\mathrm{emit}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal A}\quad\wedge\quad \forall T\in\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{emit}):\ \mathrm{emit}\circ\mathrm{rebuild}(T)=T$$
if and only if the $n$ field faithfully encodes subtree structure (no truncation, no ambiguous anchoring).

*Proof*. The uniqueness of post-order traversal guarantees that emit is injective: given the post-order sequence and the arity $n$ of each node, bottom-up stack reconstruction is deterministic — upon reading a row $(z,r,n)$, the top of the stack holds exactly the $n$ already-reconstructed subtrees of that node (post-order guarantees children precede the parent). Determinism of rebuild + structure preservation of emit ⟹ they are mutually inverse. Conversely, if $n$ is truncated (e.g. $n>255$ truncated by u8) or anchored ambiguously (see the three defect classes of §9.2), the stack depth misaligns and rebuild outputs $\bot$ or a wrong tree. □

**Significance (the fixed-point criterion of compiler correctness)**. The round-trip consistency
$$f(T)=\mathrm{emit}(\mathrm{rebuild}(T))=T$$
is a direct instance of Kleene's fixed-point theorem at the IR layer: **compiler correctness ⟺ the IR round-trip is a fixed point**. This compresses the judgment of "whether semantics is preserved" into a single machine comparison — no axiomatic semantics needed, no proof obligation, no dependence on test coverage. Qualification: this criterion verifies **structural fidelity** (lossless transcription); semantic correctness additionally requires the correctness of the operator implementation (borne by the six-operator axiom layer, §2.1) — the criterion shifts the burden of correctness from "the infinite program space" to "the finite operator base", which is its substantive contribution.

**Strengthened V1′ (deep fixed point — the iterative version of the criterion)** [VERIFIED]. Theorem V1's round-trip fixed point $f(T)=T$ is a **single-step** criterion. The engineering implementation gives a stronger **iterative** version: let
$$F=\mathrm{emit}\circ\mathrm{rebuild},\qquad f=\mathrm{rebuild}\circ\mathrm{emit}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal A}$$
then for any number of rounds $N$,
$$F^{N}(T)=T\quad\text{and}\quad \mathrm{fp}\big(F^{N}(T)\big)=\mathrm{fp}(T)$$
where $\mathrm{fp}$ is the whole-table cryptographic fingerprint (collapsing the entire table into one GF$(p)$ element, so "are two tables identical" degenerates into one integer comparison). **After 20 rounds of iteration the residueon table is pole-for-pole invariant and the fingerprint is invariant** (golden fingerprints $2722$ and $541$ fixed throughout).

*Significance*. (i) A single-step fixed point only captures "are emission and reconstruction mutually inverse"; the iterative fixed point additionally captures **accumulated drift** — if some round has an $\varepsilon$-level asymmetry (e.g. field truncation lost one-sidedly in the round trip), a single-step comparison may miss it because $T$ happens to sit on the fixed point, whereas iteration necessarily amplifies it. (ii) The iterative criterion advances Theorem V3 (existence of the finite-field fixed point) from "existence" to "an observable stable plateau": the fixed point is not an abstract limit but the very table still present at round 20. (iii) The fingerprint fixed point is **cheap continuous monitoring** — one integer comparison per compilation, not $m$ pole-by-pole comparisons.

**Self-describing poles and bijection compatibility** [VERIFIED]. After V13 introduces the domain marker $z{=}31$ ($r{=}$ the prime itself, [Paper4] Corollary 23.4), the residueon table is no longer a mere image of the syntax tree but carries domain information. Theorem V1 **still holds** under this extension: the domain marker itself is a $(z,r,n)$ triple ($n{=}1$, a leaf), so stack reconstruction treats it as an ordinary node, the round trip is unchanged, and the property is fixed as a regression criterion. **This is a stress test of the single-IR principle (§3.3)**: a new language capability (cross-domain execution) introduces no new IR type but reuses the existing triple — a direct verification of the canonical constraint "zero syntax addition, IR-internal extension".

**Difference from compressed encodings**. The residueon table is **not** a compressed encoding of the syntax tree (zip/tree-serialization), but an **isomorphic image**: each node's type, data, and structural depth occupy a dedicated semantic slot. The round trip of a compressed encoding depends on decompression and compression being mutually inverse (an algorithm-layer convention); the round trip of an isomorphic image depends on mathematical structure (the faithfulness of the $n$ field) — the former is an engineering contract, the latter a theorem (V1).

**Multi-pole configurations: the representation layer does not depend on the superposition principle** [observation, representation layer]. [Paper1] Proposition 5.1 maps, under topological approximation, the singularity structure of the mother equation's vortex solutions to the pole configuration of the residue field (vortex core ↔ pole, topological charge ↔ pole order; the non-analyticity of the radial envelope is explicitly ignored — a CGL vortex solution contains an $|z|$-type radial factor, is not holomorphic, its reciprocal is not meromorphic, and since CGL is a nonlinear dissipative equation, multi-vortex solutions cannot be written as superpositions or products of single-vortex solutions). The residueon table here offers a **representation-layer way out**: multi-vortex configuration = multi-pole configuration = a **list** of $(z,r,n)$ triples — each core's winding number enters the $n$ field, the configuration enters the list structure, and hierarchical composition (vortex molecules/bound states) enters the nesting structure (three-level nested bootstrap has already proven that tables may contain tables, §5). The key point: **representing multiple poles needs no superposition principle** — the residueon table encodes the singularity configuration itself, not a product of solutions; the full complexity of the nonlinear dynamics is handed over to the table's evolution operator (algebraic execution of the six operators over GF$(p)$), and algebraic execution has no "superposition failure" problem — the field has no linear-superposition structure that must hold in the first place. This is **exactly compatible** with Proposition 5.1's topological approximation: the residueon table faithfully carries precisely the singularity skeleton that the topological approximation retains. The dynamics layer (whether table evolution reproduces vortex interactions) is unmeasured; listed as O42.

**Time-reversal duality reading** [PROPOSAL]. If compilation (post-order emission) is read as forward time evolution, then decompilation (stack reconstruction) is a time-reversal operation, and the Kleene bijection is the **Kramers condition** of the compilation layer: inverting twice returns exactly to the origin — at the bit level, stronger than quantum Kramers ($T^2=-1$, off by a phase). The pathology of classical decompilation is rooted in the compilation map's non-injectivity (information loss); the residueon table's triple semantics guarantees emission is injective, so time reversal is well-defined. "Time-reversal symmetry" migrates from a physical property (the Frobenius correspondence of collision V) to a data-structure property — two representations of the same object (AST ↔ residueon table) are mutual time reversals. ### 3.3 The Single-IR Principle

**Principle (single IR)**. The whole compilation system admits exactly one intermediate representation: the standard residueon configuration specification (pole-position array + residue-value array + metadata, i.e. a $(z,r,n)$ table + header). Token sequences, AST, QIR, WASM opcodes, and machine code are **projections** of the residueon table at different stages, not independent IRs.

The rigidity of this principle comes from [Paper4] §3.5's syntactic isomorphism and this paper's Theorem V1: since all syntactic/instructional objects are already losslessly embedded in the residueon table, introducing a second IR introduces a second semantic undertaking and the homogeneity crisis returns unchanged. This forms a methodological opposition to the classical multi-IR cascade (AST→HIR→MIR→LIR→bytecode): **the classical pipeline trades multiple IR layers for per-layer optimization freedom; this framework trades a single IR for decidable correctness and backend homogeneity**. The cost: optimization must be expressed as algebraic transforms on the residueon table (Part Six), without the convenient syntax of per-IR layers — a deliberate trade-off.

### 3.4 Post-Order Emission and Stack Reconstruction: the Algorithm

Post-order emission: each node is transcribed post-order into one row $(z,r,n)$; stack reconstruction: leaves push $r$, an $n$-ary row pops $n$ items and assembles them by $z$; round-trip check: emission followed by reconstruction equals the original table, i.e. a fixed point.

(Stack reconstruction is implemented on the ΨLang side by the reconstruction channel of the core compiler module.) Stack reconstruction costs $O(m)$ ($m$ = table length), and the round-trip check costs $O(m)$. The tower iteration of self-referential verification (§4.3) invokes the round-trip bijection check once per level, with cost linear in the compiler's own size.

# Part Two: Bootstrap Theory

## 5 The Rigorous Definition of Self-Bootstrapping

### 5.1 Definition and the Three Constraints

**Theorem V2 (the rigorous definition of self-bootstrapping)**. Self-bootstrapping = a compiler $C$ implemented as a residueon table (one residueon table) takes its own residueon table as input (the data $C$), passes through ALU collision convergence (the action of the six operators), and outputs a new residueon table $C'$ with $C'$ **bit-identical** to $C$. None of the three constraints is dispensable:

1. **Constraint 1 (the input must be "myself")**: the compiler's input is its own table, not an arbitrary program — this is the entry of self-reference;
2. **Constraint 2 (the output must be bit-identical)**: not "semantically equivalent", not "behaviorally consistent", but bit-for-bit equal — this is the source of decidability;
3. **Constraint 3 (the compiler must "understand" residueon syntax)**: $C$ internally contains the full capability to parse the $(z,r,n)$ structure, distinguish data from opcodes, execute six-operator transforms, and generate a new table — this excludes the trivial "table lookup and re-read" fixed point.

### 5.2 The Candidate Criterion: Transcription ≠ Bootstrap

**Proposition (candidate body)**. Transcribing the ΨLang compiler into a residueon table does not equal self-bootstrapping — it merely **manufactures a candidate body of self-bootstrapping**. Genuine self-bootstrapping is: this residueon table, in the ALU, takes itself as input, passes through collision, and outputs a residueon table bit-identical to itself.

This criterion excludes the first class of pseudo-bootstrap: any program can be serialized into data (Gödel encoding), and serialization per se has no self-referential content; the self-referential content lies in the closure that "the serialized program can read and rewrite its own serialization".

### 5.3 Against Linear Reduction: Why $g=32769$ Is Not Bootstrap

Over GF$(p)$ there exist linear-reduction factors (e.g. $g=32769$ making a specific linear transform reduce in one step), but:

**Proposition (against linear reduction)**. Bootstrap is not "reduction" but "self-replication" — the compiler outputs not "a number" but **an entire residueon table**. The compiler $C$ must contain the capability to "understand the residueon-table structure": parse the $(z,r,n)$ structure, distinguish data from opcodes, execute six-operator transforms, and generate a bit-identical new table. This is not something a single number $g$ can accomplish; this is the self-referential structure of the whole table.

Formal statement: let the compiler be a map $C:\mathcal T\to\mathcal T$ (a self-map of the residueon-table space). Linear reduction handles only the restriction of $C$ to a one-dimensional subspace; self-bootstrapping requires $C(C)=C$, where the outer $C$ acts as a function (must be fully executed) and the inner $C$ acts as data (must be fully parsed). **The dual undertaking of function bit and data bit** is the essence of self-bootstrapping, and what distinguishes it from every non-self-referential fixed point.

| Criterion | $\mathrm{compile}(\mathrm{compiler.}\psi)=\mathrm{compiler.}\psi$ (textual bit-identity) | $C(C)=C$ (algebraic fixed point) |

| Abstraction leak | yes (textual format depends on the host) | none (field elements have no format) |
| Positioning | closure at the language level | existence at the physical level |
| Status | achieved (bootstrap-chain engineering) | achieved (residue-field closure, §9) |

The two do not conflict but stratify: textual self-hosting is the **simulation-layer verification** of physical self-bootstrapping — when the ΨLang compiler rewrites itself in ΨLang and reproduces bit-for-bit, it proves "this operator set suffices to carry compilation semantics"; physical self-bootstrapping further requires that "the carrier itself is an algebraic object". The rigorous version of the analogy: **first-generation bootstrap is writing a C compiler in assembly; second-generation bootstrap is rewriting the C compiler in C; textual self-hosting corresponds to the second generation, and physical self-bootstrapping is the third — the compiler's form of existence is homogeneous with its object of operation**.

## 6 The Three Tiers of Self-Bootstrapping and the Cap

### 6.1 The Object Level (first-order bootstrap)

$$C\cdot C=C$$
The compiler takes its own source as input and produces itself — language-level closure. Product: trustworthy instruction-set closure (the compiler can regenerate itself without an external compiler). Engineering counterpart: the ΨLang bootstrap chain (seed build → self-compiling build → pre-compiled AOT).

### 6.2 The Meta Level (second-order bootstrap)

$$\exists\ \Psi^{(2)}\ \text{s.t.}\ \Psi^{(2)}\cdot\Psi^{(2)}=\Psi^{(2)}\ \wedge\ \pi(\Psi^{(2)})=C\ \wedge\ \Psi^{(2)}\neq\iota(C)$$
where $\pi$ is the projection (the object-level compiler compiled out by the meta-system) and $\iota$ is the embedding. **The crux is $\Psi^{(2)}\neq\iota(C)$**: if the meta-system merely "mentions" $C$, it is only an annotation of first-order bootstrap. The meta-system of second-order bootstrap must **itself be** residue-field computation — the compiler is no longer implemented as an instruction stream but as field functions (§10): lexing = field convolution, parsing = topological simplification of meta-operators, semantics = algebraic identities, codegen = algebraic identity transforms.

The residueon semantics of first-order and second-order bootstrap differ: **the residue of the first bootstrap is "a value", the residue of the second bootstrap is "the marker of the result of applying an operation to $G_0$"**. The operating program of second-order bootstrap: freeze the first-bootstrap artifact as the immutable mathematical object $G_0$ (the Genesis Block, computing its topological fingerprint: Lyapunov spectrum / homology-group generators / Galois orbits / algebraic entropy), then rebuild from the field axioms.

### 6.3 The Evolution Level (third-order bootstrap) and "the third tier has no independent fixed point"

**Two corrections** (in the evolution history of the framework, worth recording as methodology): the first definition of "third-order bootstrap = domain library" is corrected to "**the domain projection of second-order bootstrap**" — the domain library runs through the second-order interface and does not bootstrap the kernel; **the third tier has no independent Kleene fixed point**. The genuine third bootstrap is redefined as the **evolution level**:

$$\text{evolution-level bootstrap: use a Darwin engine to automatically evolve a better compiler, base library, and even the evolution engine itself}$$

The human defines only three things: the gene encoding (the mutable parametrization of the compiler), the mutation rules (§20 Galois mutation), and the selection criterion (Lyapunov stability + pole count + round-trip consistency). Acceptance criterion: the evolved compiler, compiling its own source, outputs a configuration identical to itself (fixed-point preservation), with the core pole count reduced by more than 30% relative to the human implementation.

### 6.4 The Capping Theorem: There Is No Fourth Order

**Proposition V4b (bootstrap cap)** [argument, non-formal theorem]. There is no fourth-order bootstrap. Upward, a so-called "fourth-order bootstrap" is essentially the recursive application of the third order (an evolution engine evolving an evolution engine — still self-nesting within the evolution level); downward, digging further reaches the field axioms (the nine field axioms are indivisible atoms). **This is already the boundary drawn by Gödel's incompleteness theorem**: any sufficiently strong self-referential system has an intrinsic upper bound on its self-descriptive capability; a "higher-order bootstrap" beyond that bound either collapses into a composition of lower orders or degenerates into a restatement of the axiom layer.

### 6.5 Demotion, Not Abandonment

First-order bootstrap (the instruction-stream compilation chain) is not abandoned after second-order bootstrap completes, but **demoted**: from "the compiler proper" to "the **host simulation layer** of residue-field computation on x86/WASM" (the training wheels), on par with the photonic and FPGA backends. First-order bootstrap thereafter only does "translation", not "compilation". Canonical statement: **the CPU merely simulates residue-field evolution; the compiler's logic itself must be field computation — it runs directly on photonic hardware too**. This is simultaneously an anti-fraud criterion: implementing a "residue-field compiler" with CPU instructions and then claiming second-order bootstrap is a substitution of concepts.

## 7 Finite-Field Fixed-Point Theory

### 7.1 Theorem V3: Existence and Termination

**Theorem V3 (finite-field fixed point)**. Let $C:\mathcal T\to\mathcal T$ be a compiler self-map on the residueon-table space, with $\dim\mathcal T=m$ (table-length upper bound). Then: (i) Kleene's fixed-point theorem guarantees the constructive existence of the least fixed point (the chain $C(\bot)\le C^2(\bot)\le\dots$ from $\bot$); (ii) by the finiteness of GF$(p)$, $|\mathcal T|=p^{3m}$, so iteration must enter a cycle within $p^{3m}$ steps; (iii) bit-identical convergence ($C^{k+1}=C^k$) is the collapse of the cycle into a fixed point.

*Proof*. (i) is the standard form of Kleene's theorem ($\mathcal T$ a finite partial order, $C$ order-preserving — the structural monotonicity of the emission function). (ii) pigeonhole. (iii) the cycle has length 1 if and only if $C^{k+1}=C^k$. □

**Significance and boundary**. The finiteness of the finite field turns "does recursion terminate" from undecidable (the Turing-machine case) into **boundedly decidable**: $p^{3m}$ is astronomical, but its existence guarantees "no infinite loop at the decision level" — any actual infinite loop can only be an implementation defect of $C$ (e.g. non-convergent gear shifting, §14.4), not a mathematical openness. Applicability boundary: the termination bound $p^{3m}$ has no engineering significance; actual convergence is borne by tower-iteration measurement (§9); the monotonicity assumption of (i) depends on the post-order structure of the emission function, and if optimization passes introduce non-monotone reordering, it must be argued separately under the group-action framework of §15.

### 7.2 Comparison with Kleene's Recursion Theorem

Kleene's classical recursion theorem guarantees: for any computable functional $F$, there is a program $e$ with $\varphi_e=\varphi_{F(e)}$ — i.e. "a program can know its own source". This framework's fixed point is the stronger **bit level**: not merely behaviorally equivalent ($\varphi$ equal) but literally equal ($e=F(e)$). This strengthening is exactly the content of constraint 2, and the reason it is machine-decidable. The cost: a bit-level fixed point is extremely sensitive to implementation normalization (a synonymous reordering breaks bit equality), so tower iteration must normalize first (the canonical order of the fingerprint) before comparing.

### 7.3 Theorem V36: Banach Contractive Bootstrap — Uniqueness and Exponential Convergence

**Theorem V36 (bootstrap contraction map)** [VERIFIED, scale-metric layer]. Consider the bootstrap iteration $T_{k+1}=\mathrm{compile}(T_k)$ under the **scale metric** (the logarithmic distance between token count and pole count). The measured Lipschitz constant is
$$L=\frac{\mathrm{poles}}{\mathrm{tokens}}\in[0.821,0.878]<1$$
(measured over six consecutive simplified versions V12.18–V12.29: $0.869/0.878/0.849/0.850/0.852/0.821$, all strictly below 1). Then:

1. **Existence and uniqueness**: by Banach's fixed-point theorem, the bootstrap fixed point $T^\ast=\mathrm{compile}(T^\ast)$ exists and is unique under this metric;
2. **Exponential convergence**: from any initial compiler $T_0$, $\|T_n-T^\ast\|\le L^n\|T_0-T^\ast\|$; with fixed-point exponent $\alpha=-\log_2L=0.234$, the error shrinks by a factor of $L$ each round, leaving $19.7\%$ after ten rounds and $3.9\%$ after twenty;
3. **Kolmogorov-complexity fixed point**: by reversibility (Theorem V1), $K(\mathrm{IR})\le K(\mathrm{source})$ and $K(\mathrm{source})\le K(\mathrm{IR})+O(1)$, so the bootstrap artifact satisfies $K(\mathrm{compile}(T))=K(T)+O(1)$ — the bootstrap compiler is a complexity fixed point of algorithmic information theory.

**Division of labor with Theorem V3**. Theorem V3 (pigeonhole) gives **algebraic existence**: iteration over a finite field must enter a cycle within $p^{3m}$ steps — the bound has no engineering significance but excludes mathematical openness. Theorem V36 gives **analytic uniqueness and rate**: contractivity refines "enters a cycle" into "converges to a unique point" and gives an observable convergence rate. The two are two characterizations of the same fixed point: existence from the finiteness of the space, uniqueness from the contractivity of the map.

*Boundaries and limitations*. ① $L$ is an observation under the **scale metric** (output size / input size), not a Lipschitz constant under a semantic metric — contractivity characterizes "the bootstrap iteration does not inflate in size", which does not directly imply "semantic error shrinks exponentially" (semantic equivalence is borne separately by the bit-identity criterion). ② The six data points come from the simplified spectrum of the same compiler (V12.18→V12.29), i.e. **observations within one family**, not cross-family statistics; $L<1$ is stable within that family but does not constitute a universal assertion over all ΨLang programs. ③ The practical significance of convergence is the **controllability of residual simplification**: the compression ratio of the simplification operator is of the same magnitude as $L$ (the token ratio 6111→5201 gives $0.851$ versus $L=0.849$), so the simplification–bootstrap composite remains contractive — "simplification never crosses the fixed point" gains a metric-theoretic ground, complementing Proposition V31's bit-level evidence.

# Part Three: Trustworthy Compilation

## 8 Defending Against Trusting Trust: Triangular Cross-Verification

### 8.1 The Thompson Attack Model

Thompson's (1984) attack: a malicious compiler $K$ injects a backdoor while compiling itself (or a target such as login); when $K$ is recompiled from its "clean source", the backdoor re-injects itself — **the backdoor exists only in machine code, and source audit never sees it**. The essence of the attack: the trust anchor of a self-referential system cannot be the system itself.

### 8.2 Three-Compiler Positioning

**Construction** [VERIFIED, two of three corners closed]. Three paradigm-distinct compiler implementations:

| Implementation | Paradigm | Positioning | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Engineering implementation (ΨLang2, 8 modules / 24,016 lines / 311 unit tests) | systems language + WASM runtime | **golden reference** (the trust anchor) | in place |
| ΨLang version (138,623 bytes) | this framework's language | **paradigm anchor** (native second-order bootstrap) | in place |
| Residueon-native version | residueon-table direct compilation | **evolution anchor** (the ultimate third-order closure) | achieved (with fixed-point criterion) |

Verification chain: vertical single chain (level-by-level bit-identity: ΨLang output = verification-engine output) → horizontal cross-check (the triangular closure: the three parties output the same bit stream on the same input) → fixed-point convergence (each self-bootstraps) → evolution integration.

### 8.3 Proposition V5: the Trust Upper Bound

**Proposition V5 (fault-tolerance bound of triangular verification)** [argument]. Suppose the three implementations each independently contain a hidden output-changing defect with probability $\epsilon_i$. If the three parties output the same bit stream on the same input, the probability that "all three err identically" is of order $\prod_i\epsilon_i$ (requiring the defects to produce the same wrong output in a correlated manner, far below the product of the individual defect rates). Against the Thompson attack: the attack must **simultaneously infect three paradigms that share no code path** (the Rust type system / the ΨLang interpreter / the residueon-table ALU), and the infection must produce consistent bit-level output across all three paradigms — a singly-infected compiler chain is exposed at the cross-check.

*Boundaries and limitations*. ① The proposition depends on the **independence assumption**: if the three implementations share algorithmic structure, personnel, or toolchains, the $\epsilon_i$ are correlated and multiplicative suppression fails. This framework's independence is paradigm-level (three different computational carriers), but not mathematically proven independence. ② Triangular verification defends against "compile-time injection", not "operator-axiom-layer pollution" (swapping the six operators) — trust in the axiom layer is mathematical trust, not engineering trust. ③ The premise of triangular closure is full semantic alignment of the three parties. Two corners are closed (Rust verification engine ↔ ΨLang bit-identical, a measurement record from before that engine's retirement); the third corner (full residueon-native) is on the tower.

### 8.4 Methodological Significance

The moment the triangular closure forms is the moment third-order bootstrap becomes genuinely trustworthy — **three completely different implementation paradigms finally converge on the same result, essentially excluding the possibility of a hidden bug in any single implementation**. This is of the same family as Diverse Double-Compiling (DDC), but DDC must seek a second compiler externally (ecosystem contingency), whereas this framework's triangle is **endogenously constructed** (paradigm stratification is part of the framework design).

### 8.5 The Fourth Corner: the Cross-Language Independent Reader — Upgrading "Independence" from Paradigm Independence to Code Independence

The trust upper bound of Proposition V5 depends on the **(quasi-)independence** of the three compilers. This section examines that assumption and strengthens it.

**Independence audit (the soft spot of Proposition V5)**. Among the three edges of the triangle, the Rust verification engine and the ΨLang self-implementation differ in language and code, but share **the same design intent** — same author, same specification, same test suite. This is the genuine soft spot: §8.4 claims the triangle is "endogenously constructed", but endogeneity simultaneously implies the risk of **correlated failure** (the same conceptual misjudgment manifests the same way on both sides). DDC demands "independent implementations"; independence in the strict sense is unattainable in a single-person/single-team project.

**Strengthening: the fourth corner (cross-language independent reader)** [VERIFIED]. Defining the residueon table as a **cross-language serialization protocol** can introduce a genuinely independent verifier:

| Property | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Format | pure-ASCII pole table + self-describing domain annotation ([Paper4] Corollary 23.4) + fingerprint check; **no binary, no compression, no accompanying schema** |
| Reader | a zero-dependency Python script (about a hundred lines, standard-library integer arithmetic only, containing no Rust/ΨLang code of this project) |
| Verification action | read the residueon-table text → independently re-compute the whole-table fingerprint → compare with the embedded fingerprint |
| Source of independence | **zero code sharing** (different language, different author, different implementation path) + **minimal specification** (the protocol specifies only the triple and the fingerprint algorithm, no compilation semantics) |

**Proposition V5′ (four-corner trust bound, strengthened version)**. Beyond the three-compiler triangle of Proposition V5, add a **cross-language independent reader** as the fourth corner. Then:

1. **First corner (Rust verification engine)**: compile + emit the residueon-table text, embedding fingerprint $\mathrm{fp}_{\mathrm{Rust}}$;
2. **Second corner (ΨLang self-implementation)**: same input, independent emission, embedding $\mathrm{fp}_{\Psi}$;
3. **Third corner (residueon-native)**: consume the residueon-table text directly (direct execution, passing through no compiler), obtaining the execution result;
4. **Fourth corner (Python reader)**: read the first/second corner's residueon-table text and independently re-compute $\mathrm{fp}_{\mathrm{Py}}$.

If $\mathrm{fp}_{\mathrm{Rust}}=\mathrm{fp}_{\Psi}=\mathrm{fp}_{\mathrm{Py}}$ and the third corner's execution result is correct, then the entire defect class of "format misreading" is excluded — because the fourth corner **has never read any compilation code of this project**, it only reads numbers per the protocol.

*Why this is stronger than the triangle*. The first three corners verify "**do the three implementations compute the same table**"; the fourth verifies "**when a third party reads this table per its public specification, is it still the same table**". The former is **computational consistency**, the latter **representational consistency** — the subtlety of the Thompson attack is precisely that it can make computation consistent while the representation is tampered (the backdoor is not in the source but in the interpretation of the artifact). The fourth corner is so far the only verification reaching that layer.

*Boundaries and limitations*. ① The fourth corner covers only the **representation layer** (format and fingerprint), not compilation semantics (it compiles nothing); it is complementary to, not a substitute for, the first three corners. ② The fingerprint algorithm itself is shared (the fourth corner implements the same algorithm) — if the fingerprint algorithm is defective, all four corners fail together. Mitigation: the fingerprint algorithm is minimal (one GF$(p)$ polynomial evaluation, human-checkable), and is independently cross-checked by Theorem V1's structural criterion.

*Methodological positioning*. This is the third realization, at the **protocol layer**, of this framework's general route of "migrating trust from code review to arithmetic verification" (§2.4 constant layer, Theorem V1 structural layer): **operators are defined by mathematics, constants by number theory, structure by fixed points, and formats by an independently implementable ASCII protocol** — none of the four levels depends on "trusting the implementer".

### 8.6 Compile-Time Evaluation: the Compile-Time Function and an Executable Instance of the Futamura First Projection [VERIFIED]

**Proposition V17 (reversible Futamura first projection)**. Let $\mathcal A$ be the syntax-tree set and $\mathcal T$ the residueon-table set. The emission–reconstruction map pair of this paper
$$\mathrm{emit}:\mathcal A\to\mathcal T,\qquad \mathrm{rebuild}:\mathcal T\to\mathcal A$$
satisfies:

1. **Faithfulness** $\mathrm{rebuild}\circ\mathrm{emit}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal A}$ (Theorem V1);
2. **Idempotence** let $F:=\mathrm{emit}\circ\mathrm{rebuild}:\mathcal T\to\mathcal T$, then $F^2=F$ (strengthened V1′, $N{=}20$ rounds measured).

An endomorphism satisfying $F^2=F$ is a **projection**. Hence the residueon table is a **reversible specialization** over $\mathcal A$: $(\mathcal T,\mathrm{emit})$ is an instance of the Futamura first projection $\mathrm{mix}(\mathrm{interp},\mathrm{source})=\mathrm{target}$, and this instance additionally satisfies reversibility that classical partial evaluation **lacks**.

*Proof*. (2) follows from strengthened V1′'s $F^N(T)=T\ \forall N\ge1$ by taking $N=2$. (1) is Theorem V1. Combined, they give: $\mathrm{emit}$ is injective (has a left inverse by (1)), and $F$ is an idempotent surjection from $\mathcal T$ onto $\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{emit})$ — exactly the universal property of a projection. □

**Relation to classical partial-evaluation theory (the key difference)**. In the classical Futamura projection, $\mathrm{mix}$ consumes the static input $\mathrm{source}$ and produces $\mathrm{target}$, and **the source generally cannot be recovered from $\mathrm{target}$** — specialization is lossy (this is also the root of "compiled artifacts need debug information / symbol tables": the existence of ELF/DWARF compensates exactly for this loss). This framework, by condition (1), makes specialization **strictly reversible**, hence:

| | Classical Futamura / partial evaluation | This framework (reversible specialization) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathrm{source}\to\mathrm{target}$ | lossy | lossless (bijection) |
| Recover source from artifact | impossible ⟹ needs symbol table/DWARF | possible ($\mathrm{rebuild}$) ⟹ **symbol table eliminable** |
| Self-certification of artifact | none | yes (fingerprint + cross-language independent reader, §8.5) |
| Specializing again after specialization | possible (Futamura 2/3) | possible, and **idempotent** (already at the fixed point, re-specialization unchanged) |

*This difference is not an improvement but a structural advantage*: it turns "debuggability of compiled artifacts" from an **external add-on** (debug info, symbol tables, source maps) into an **intrinsic property** (the artifact itself is another representation of the source). Corollary 23.4 (domain markers eliminable) and §8.5's fourth corner (cross-language independent reader) are both realizations of this advantage at different layers.

#### 8.6.1 Binding-Time Analysis as a Maybe Monad

Proposition V17 describes the projection at the residueon-table layer; at the expression layer, the executable instance of the same idea is the compile-time evaluator, whose core is the two mutually recursive functions of the compile-time function-call evaluator and the constant-expression evaluator.

**Proposition V18 (binding-time monad)**. The return type of the compile-time evaluator has two constructors, semantically exactly the **binding-time annotations** of partial evaluation:

$$\underbrace{\text{present}(v)}_{\text{static: known at compile time}}\qquad \underbrace{\text{absent}}_{\text{dynamic: deferred to runtime}}$$

And the short-circuit propagation rule of binding time is this: if any subexpression is dynamic, the whole expression is dynamic, returning immediately. This is the standard Kleisli composition of the Maybe monad, and here its semantic content is BTA's (binding-time analysis) **divides/joins rule**: a binary-operation node is static $\iff$ the left operand is static $\wedge$ the right operand is static.

*Corollary (fallback = specialization failure)*. When the compile-time evaluator returns absent, the caller falls back to normal call-instruction emission. Hence "partial evaluation" here is not an all-or-nothing compilation mode but **per-expression** specialization — precisely what distinguishes partial evaluation from global constant folding: folding acts only on fully-static expressions, whereas partial evaluation, on mixed expressions, **keeps the dynamic part and computes the static part**. The current implementation's upper bound: when a function body contains `while`/`for`/`if`, the whole function is judged dynamic (decided by the evaluator's dynamism criterion), so static subexpressions inside loop bodies are not specialized — a **known debt**, whose repair path is the standard loop unrolling + multi-valued binding BTA extension (O17).

#### 8.6.2 Zero-Cost Assertions: the Compile-Time Realization of Proof Erasure

The code-generation stage elevates `assert` into a **three-state** structure, sharing with Proposition 7.3 (the three-channel certificate) the same criterion of "when can it be decided at compile time":

| Decidability of the condition | Handling | Runtime cost | Nature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compile-time decidable and **false** | **compilation failure** (panic at compile time) | — | proof obligation discharged at compile time |
| Compile-time decidable and **true** | emit constant zero (no-op) | **zero** | **proof erasure** |
| Compile-time **undecidable** | emit a conditional-trap instruction | one comparison | runtime check |

*Relation to dependently-typed languages*. In dependently-typed languages (Coq/Agda/Idris), proof terms of the $\mathrm{Prop}$ universe produce no runtime code after erasure; here the same principle dispatches on **decidability** rather than the type layer: statically decidable assertions are fully erased (zero cost, and failure exposed at compile time), undecidable ones degrade to runtime checks. Thus "erasure" here is guaranteed not by the type system (the $\mathrm{Prop}/\mathrm{Set}$ distinction) but by **binding-time analysis** — two different routes to zero-cost abstraction, this framework taking the latter.

*Unification with Proposition 7.3*. The three-channel certificate ([Paper4] Proposition 7.3) and the zero-cost assertion answer **the same question** — "can some property be decided at compile time, and how to fall back at runtime when it cannot": the former acts on the **value-range constraint** $\rho$ ($\mathrm{CRT}(v_1,v_2)\stackrel{?}{=}v_{\mathbb Z}$), the latter on the **logical assertion**. The two together constitute this framework's **graded decidability principle**: static first, dynamic fallback, graded declaration.

### 8.7 The Paradigm Revolution: Four Structural Differences from Existing Compiler Paradigms

The preceding subsections gave the mechanisms item by item; this section consolidates them into **differences from existing compiler paradigms**, each with a checkable discriminating criterion — a paradigm difference that is not checkable is mere rhetoric.

| Dimension | Mainstream paradigm (LLVM / GCC / JVM / Truffle) | This framework | Discriminating criterion (checkable) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **① Status of the IR** | the IR is an **intermediate** representation: front-end output, back-end input, optimization on top of it; the IR itself is not executable or deployable | the IR **is the artifact**: the residueon table is directly executable, directly serializable (textual format), directly transcribable to hardware (Proposition V15) | Can the IR be executed directly? Can a run result be obtained without any backend? |
| **② Reversibility of compilation** | compilation is lossy: source unrecoverable from artifact, hence symbol tables / DWARF / source maps | compilation is **reversible**: $\mathrm{rebuild}\circ\mathrm{emit}=\mathrm{id}$, artifact self-certifying (Proposition V17) | Can the AST be rebuilt from the artifact with a bit-identical round trip? |
| **③ Source of correctness** | testing + formal verification (CompCert-style: proof assistants prove semantics preservation); the compiler body is **trusted** | **constructive**: bit-identity of three independent implementations + a cross-language independent reader (fourth corner); the compiler body is **cross-verified** | Do two code-disjoint paths yield the same bits? |
| **④ Status of arithmetic** | arithmetic is a capability of the target machine (float/integer defined by hardware, compiler adapts) | arithmetic is an **axiom**: GF$(p)$ given by the six operators, backends adapt; no float, no randomness, no undefined behavior | Does switching backends change the result? (this framework: no) |

**The mutual-support relations of the four differences** (the reason they form a "paradigm" rather than a "feature list"):

- ② depends on ①: precisely because the residueon table is an artifact rather than an intermediate state, it must (and can) be reversible — an intermediate representation has no need to be reversible.
- ③ depends on ②: precisely because of reversibility, the comparison of two paths has a **common canonical form** (the residueon table), making bit-identity decidable; with a lossy IR, "are two paths the same" can only be compared on the final output, at a much coarser granularity.
- ④ depends on ①: precisely because arithmetic is an axiom rather than a machine capability, the IR can keep its semantics detached from any concrete backend, making ①'s "IR as artifact" hold.

*The reverse-side costs (must be recorded together)*. Each of the four differences has a cost, and the costs share a common source — **the strong constraints of the axiom layer buy backend independence while losing arithmetic-layer customizability**:

1. Rejecting floating point means rejecting fixed-point within the GF channel (§19.3) — `3*65536\to65534`, and the finite field cannot be ordered; the cost is absorbed by channel migration (`<<`, `/`, `%`, comparison go through the integer sub-language), but it is not zero-cost: cross-domain values must explicitly switch channels;
2. The six operators being the sole arithmetic primitives of the GF channel makes all tricks of "tampering at the arithmetic layer of that channel" unavailable (bit operations and integer division are unaffected, belonging to the integer sub-language);
3. No randomness and no undefined behavior make algorithms that rely on randomness or UB optimization (randomized quicksort, aggressive UB optimization) inexpressible;
4. Reversibility requires the IR to keep full structural information, making certain size optimizations (dropping type information, merging unreachable code) conflict with bijection — see O18 for the trade-off rules.

*Positioning against existing work*. This framework makes no claim of "being comprehensively better than LLVM/GCC/CompCert" — in optimization depth, ecosystem scale, and target-platform count, it is far from their magnitude. What this paper claims is **a different design point**: on the three dimensions of "determinism, verifiability, and software-hardware boundary unification", the residueon-table paradigm provides guarantees that the mainstream paradigm **structurally does not** (because the mainstream IR is lossy and its arithmetic depends on the target machine). The relation between the two is **different design points**, not replacement. Comparable work includes: CompCert (formally verified compiler correctness, but still a one-way lossy IR), Truffle/Graal (AST interpreter + specialization, closest to this paper's Futamura viewpoint, but specialization is irreversible), RPython/PyPy (meta-circular + partial evaluation, of the same family as the bootstrap tower), and proof-carrying code (certificates carried with artifacts, of the same family as the fourth corner but relying on external verifiers).

#### 9.1.1 The Separation of Determinism from Reproducibility: a Bootstrap-Archaeology Observation [VERIFIED, factual record]

### 9.2 The u8 Order Restriction: Three Defect Cases

The $n$ field is stored as u8 (0–255), causing three classes of round-trip failure, all localized and fixed [VERIFIED]:

1. **$n$ truncation**: when a nested source segment exceeds 255 characters, the string node's $n$ field truncates → round-trip misalignment (12254 vs 12255 poles) → fix: re-slice by lines to ≤245 lines;
2. **Child-count overflow**: when a sequence node has too many children (405), $n$ overflows → fix: merge "definition and call" into pairs, anchored on the token characters near the function position (using 5 greedy anchor data points);
3. **Literal reuse misalignment**: a single literal string reuses the segment count of the previous token → parse misalignment (the literal append counter was not cleared) → fix: number literals independently.

The common lesson of the three defect classes: **Theorem V1's "faithful encoding of the $n$ field" is the lifeline of the bijection** — any engineering compromise of $n$ (truncation/overflow/reuse) directly breaks stack reconstruction, and the round-trip comparison catches it immediately. This is precisely the value of the criterion: defects are found not by test cases but are **necessarily exposed** by the fixed-point structure.

**Continuation: the five defect classes of the semantic layer (caught by dual-engine bit-identity)**. The three defect classes belong to the **structural layer** (breaking $n$-faithfulness, caught by the round trip). A further class of defects **does not break the round trip but breaks semantics** — caught by dual-engine bit-identity, forming the second line of defense:

4. **Shift asymmetry of the keyword fallback branch**: in the reference implementation the cursor first adds one then subtracts one (net effect: in place), while the ΨLang side genuinely steps back one → the assignment sign is parsed twice. **Lesson**: logically "identical-looking" code in two languages can have different side-effect semantics — bit-identity is the only reliable arbiter.
5. **A same-level operator chain emits only one BinOp**: `a+g+f+c+o` is right-associatively folded, emitting only one `+` (should emit four). **Lesson**: the implementation of precedence/associativity must agree with the **node count** of the Pratt chain, not merely the final result — bit-identity compares pole sequences, not final values, so this class of "result happens to be right" error is also caught.
6. **The modulo-instruction perpetually-true defect**: in `if m < 0 - (b//2)`, the `0 - b//2` is **GF subtraction** = $65537 - b//2$ (always greater than any non-negative $m$), so the condition is perpetually false → collatz recurses infinitely. **Lesson**: this is an instance of **domain semantics leaking into control flow** (a sibling problem of [Paper4] Corollary 22.5's context-sensitive semantics) — GF has no negative numbers; `0 - x` is not "negation" but "complement to the modulus". Bit-identity here catches a **semantic misunderstanding**, not an encoding error.

**The division of labor of the two lines of defense**:

| Line | What it catches | Criterion | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First: Kleene bijection (structural layer) | $n$ truncation/overflow/reuse | $F(T)=T$ | structural infidelity (the three defect classes of §9.2) |
| Second: dual-engine bit-identity (semantic layer) | shift asymmetry, missing node count, domain-semantics leakage | $E_1(P)=E_2(P)$ | structural fidelity but semantic drift (defects 4–6) |

*Methodology*. One line of defense is insufficient: defects 4–6 all **pass** the Kleene bijection (the table is still a valid image of the tree), and only bit-identity catches them; conversely the three defect classes of §9.2 collapse at the structural layer without needing bit-identity. The two lines are **orthogonal and both necessary** — this is the jurisdiction of D.6 (Proposition V14), "bit-identity = divergence localizer": the break of bit-identity precisely localizes the representational difference of the two implementations.

$$ (\sigma * \kappa_j)(z_0)=\sum_{u}\sigma(u)\kappa_j(z_0-u)\ \text{attains a local maximum above threshold} $$

# Part Five: The Type System — Domain-Order Inference

## 12 DOI: the Domain-Order Inference System

[Paper4] §12.9–12.10 gives the mathematical layer of domain-order theory (Theorem 23 lazy field extension, Theorem 24 k-depth hierarchy, a formal-system embryo). This section lands it as a **compile-time algorithm**: input an expression, output a domain-order annotation $\tau@k$ for every subexpression.

**Theorem V7 (domain-order inference)**. (i) The deductive closure of the seven inference rules (below) is equivalent to the lazy field-extension strategy of [Paper4] Theorem 23 — the inferred $k$ is exactly the minimal domain order $k^*$ required by Theorem 24; (ii) inference executes in compile-time polynomial time (each (Root) rule costs $O(\omega(n))$ modular exponentiations, §12.3); (iii) domain-order inference is structurally isomorphic to Hindley–Milner type inference, with classical type inference as its $k=0$ special case.

### 12.1 Syntax and Judgments

$$e ::= x\ \mid\ c\ \mid\ e_1\ op\ e_2\ \mid\ \sqrt[n]{e}\ \mid\ \mathrm{domain}(e,p)$$
The judgment $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau@k$: expression $e$ has type $\tau$, residing at domain order $k$ ($k=0$ the base field GF$(p)$, $k$ the extension degree).

### 12.2 The Seven Inference Rules

| Rule | Premise | Conclusion | Semantics |
|---|---|---|---|
| (Var) | $\Gamma(x)=\tau@k$ | $\Gamma\vdash x:\tau@k$ | variable takes the environment order |
| (Const) | $c\in GF(p)$ | $\Gamma\vdash c:GF(p)@0$ | constants live in the base field |
| (Op) | $\Gamma\vdash e_i:\tau_i@k_i$, $op$ well-defined in $F_{\mathrm{lcm}(k_1,k_2)}$ | $\Gamma\vdash e_1\,op\,e_2:\tau@\mathrm{lcm}(k_1,k_2)$ | operation takes the least-common-multiple field |
| (Root) | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau@k$, $x^n-e$ irreducible over $F_k$ | $\Gamma\vdash\sqrt[n]{e}:F_{k+1}@k+1$ | **irreducible ⟹ raise order** |
| (Embed) | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau@k$, $k'\ge k$ | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau@k'$ | embedding (raise) |
| (Project) | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau@k$, $k'\le k$, projection exists | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau@k'$ | projection (lower) |
| (Auto) | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau@k$, $k^*=\min\{m\le k\mid e\in F_m\}$ | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau@k^*$ | minimization fallback |

**Note ((Auto)'s idempotence — a projection-operator reading)**. The (Auto) rule is a **projection** to the minimal domain order: once an expression reaches its minimal order, reapplying (Auto) changes no annotation — i.e. $\mathrm{auto}\circ\mathrm{auto}=\mathrm{auto}$ (idempotent). This is not coincidence but definitional necessity: a minimization operator is naturally an idempotent projection (just as rounding twice equals rounding once). The convergence of domain-order inference thus gains a double guarantee: the finite-field termination of Theorem V3 (existence) and projection idempotence (stability) — iterative inference must stop at the least fixed point and cannot oscillate.

**Note (correction: the dividend of [Paper4] Theorem 23′)**. The first draft took $\max(k_1,k_2)$ for (Op) — correct only under the inclusion chain ($k_1\mid k_2$), a remnant of "gear thinking"; the divisibility lattice gives composite degree $=\mathrm{lcm}(k_1,k_2)$ (minimal counterexample: adding a $GF(3^2)$ element to a $GF(3^3)$ element must land in $GF(3^6)$; the group-theoretic disproof is in [Paper4] §12.9). Changed to lcm throughout, at the cost of one gcd.

### 12.3 The Computable Criterion of (Root)

The crux of AutoGear is that **irreducibility is decidable and cheap**. The classical criterion (Lidl–Niederreiter Theorem 3.75):
$$x^n-a\ \text{irreducible over}\ GF(q)\iff \forall\,\ell\mid n\ (\ell\ \text{prime}):\ a^{(q-1)/\ell}\neq1,\ \text{and if}\ 4\mid n\ \text{then}\ a^{(q-1)/4}\neq-1$$
Note: the shorthand criterion of the form $a^{(q-1)/d}\neq1$ ($d=\gcd(n,q-1)$) is correct only when $n$ is prime; for composite $n$ one must test **each prime factor** $\ell$ of $n$ separately (e.g. for $n=4$, $x^4-2$ is reducible over $GF(17)$ yet $2^{(17-1)/4}=16\neq1$). Each (Root) rule needs $O(\omega(n))$ modular exponentiations ($\omega(n)$ the number of prime factors of $n$, easily affordable at compile time). Hence both (Root) and (Auto) are mechanically executable.

### 12.4 The Three-Layer Flow

1. **Constraint collection**: scan the expression; each $\sqrt[n]{a}$ contributes the constraint $n\mid p-1$ (an $n$-th root needs the $n$-th root of unity in the field);
2. **Constraint solving**: merge into $\mathrm{lcm}\mid p-1$, find the smallest satisfying prime. **Case**: the constraint $\{5\mid p-1,\ 4\mid p-1\}$ gives $\mathrm{lcm}=20$, minimal solution $p=41$ — the domain-order inference engine, having never been fed any Ramsey knowledge, discovers the Ramsey-critical prime ([Paper4] §12.10's structural explanation: 41 is the largest prime for which self-complement symmetry is available). This is an independent signal of the type system "discovering mathematics";
3. **Partition routing**: route different subexpressions to different prime fields, with CRT gluing (must satisfy the dual constraint of [Paper4] Corollary 22.2 — value range and order simultaneously matched).

## 13 Shell Polymorphism

### 13.1 Definition

$$\mathrm{id}:\ \forall k.\ (\tau@k)\to(\tau@k)$$
The identity function (and every function not touching the field structure) is polymorphic over every shell $k$: it is the same function on $F_0$, on $F_1$, on $F_5$ (the same residueon table, interpreted at different domain orders). This aligns formally with HM's let polymorphism ($\forall\alpha.\alpha\to\alpha$), but quantifies a different object: HM quantifies **types** (set structure), shell polymorphism quantifies **carrier fields** (algebraic structure).

### 13.2 Semantics and Instantiation

Instantiation of shell polymorphism = choosing a concrete $k$ (decided at compile time by the call context's domain-order constraints, §12.4); its generic body = one residueon table + one domain-order constraint predicate. **The essential difference from generics**: classical generics instantiate by generating different code (template expansion / monomorphization), whereas shell-polymorphic instantiation **does not change a single bit** — the same table, interpreted in different fields. This provides the type-layer basis for "compile once, run in multiple domains" (with [Paper4]'s mosaic partitioning).

### 13.3 Zeno Detection

The detection mechanism against infinite raising: if the domain-order trajectory $0\to1\to2\to\dots$ of the expression $e=\sqrt{2}{\sqrt{2}{\sqrt{2}{\cdots}}}$ fails to converge (ZenoDivergence), it is analogous to HM's occurs check ($\alpha=\alpha\to\beta$ fails to converge). The two are **the same structural failure** (a constraint system without a least fixed point) — see §14.4's unified geometry.

### 14.2 The Parsing Trajectory

### 14.3 The Compilation-Quality Criterion Q

Let the trajectory's domain-order peak be $k_{\max}$ and the gear-shift complexity (total variation) be $G=\sum_i|k_i-k_{i-1}|$. The two are **mutually independent**: $(a+b)\times(c+d)$ and $a+(b+(c+d))$ both have total variation 8, with domain-order peaks 2 and 4 respectively — $k_{\max}$ is a capacity metric, $G$ a switching metric. Compilation quality:
$$Q=\alpha G+\beta\sum_i\frac{1}{\mathrm{margin}_i}+\gamma C,\qquad \mathrm{margin}(e)=|\mathrm{required}_k(e)-\mathrm{current}_k(e)|$$
Large margin = the cross-section far from the Julia boundary (robust); small margin = close to the boundary, crossing back and forth (fragile). **A good optimization pass should improve both $G$ and margin**.[Paper4] has declared: $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ are uncalibrated, and the continuization of margin is pending — this paper adds the engineering meaning: the three terms of $Q$ correspond respectively to **switching cost, robustness, computational cost**, providing a first-principles objective function for pass ordering (replacing the classical profile heuristic).

then rising to the $k$-th tower level needs at least a modulus $\ge p_{\min}(k)$, hence
$$\mathrm{cost}(0\to k)\ \ge\ \log_2 p_{\min}(k)\ \text{bit}$$

**Supplement two: the channel-scoped fixed-point view — undefined in the GF channel, natively feasible in the ℤ channel and the Q64 domain** [VERIFIED, on-machine measurement]. The realizability of the fixed-point numeric view on the residueon table depends on the **execution channel**, not the architecture itself. This framework's earlier record gave the negative result "Q16.16 fixed-point is architecturally infeasible"; that conclusion is **too broad**, corrected here into a channel-scoped judgment, with evidence.

*Channel one (the GF channel): undefined, and rooted in the absence of order structure*. Q16.16 encoding requires $v_{\text{encoded}}=\mathrm{round}(v\times2^{16})$. In the GF channel, the source-level `+`, `-`, `*` **are indeed all GF operators** (the six operators are the sole arithmetic primitives of that channel, §2.1), so `num * 65536` is not "multiply by $2^{16}$" but "multiply by $65536\equiv-1$ in GF$(65537)$":

$$3\times 65536\ \xrightarrow{\ \odot\ }\ 3\odot65536=65534\ (\equiv-3)\ \neq\ 196608$$

But the surface fact (multiplication is GF) is not yet the root. The true structural reason is: **there is no order on GF$(p)$ compatible with arithmetic** — a finite field cannot be an ordered field, and comparing finite-field elements via $a<b$ is undefined. The semantics of fixed-point representation (magnitude comparison, rounding to nearest, overflow saturation) **presupposes order**; without order, fixed-point is **undefined** within the GF channel, not merely "hard to implement". This negative result still holds within its channel and is independently re-computable.

*Channel two (the ℤ channel and the Q64 domain): natively feasible*. This framework is not a single-channel architecture (three-domain execution, §2.2; domain-order inference selects the field by literal type). Operator dispatch is **layered**: `+`, `-`, `*` map to the GF operators ⊕ and ⊙, whereas **shifts `<<`/`>>`, bitwise `&`/`|`, integer division `/`, modulo `%`, and all comparison operators are native integer operations, not reduced through GF**. Hence the fixed-point encoding expression is a native expression in the integer sub-language:

$$\mathrm{fix}(n,d)=(n\ll F)/d$$

where $F$ is the scaling bit count, and $\ll$ and $/$ fall within the integer sub-language, so $(3\ll31)/2=3\cdot2^{30}$ corresponds strictly to the rational value $3/2$. This expression is implemented in **pure ΨLang** in the standard library's rational module (the scaling routine), and is one of the module's ten self-checks, independently re-computable. The value of $F$ is constrained only by the host word length: $F{=}31$ corresponds to Q32.32 on i64, $F{=}64$ to Q64.64, semantically identical, with extending $F$ depending only on landing multi-word integers.

If the third corner (residueon-native) of triangular verification (§8) is achieved, its trust structure is superior to DDC (endogenous triangle vs ecosystem-contingent dual corner).

### 23.3 Trusting-Trust Countermeasures

Thompson (1984) posed the attack; DDC (Diverse Double-Compiling, Wheeler) gives the dual-compiler cross-verification countermeasure. This paper's triangular verification (§8) is a three-paradigm strengthening of DDC (Rust/ΨLang/residueon-table, three computational carriers, not two compiler implementations), and the choice of the third corner is not ecosystem contingency but framework design (§8.4). Boundary: both DDC and this paper depend on the (quasi-)independence of implementations.

### 23.4 Dataflow Programming (FBP/LabVIEW/flow-based)

The dataflow paradigm takes **graphs** as the computation carrier — nodes + directed edges. This framework takes **fields**: a graph is discrete topology (edges are connection relations), a field is continuous superposition (all poles act on the evaluation point simultaneously). Engineering difference: dataflow is "pushed" (tokens flow along edges), the residue field "moves itself" (the evaluation point collapses the whole field); CRT parallelism needs explicit sharding in dataflow, but is natural block-diagonal in the field ([Paper4] Theorem 14 native parallelism). Theoretical difference: FBP has no algebraic invariant (graph isomorphism is the only structure), the residue field has the triple invariant cross-ratio/PCD/homology (§16.2).

### 23.5 Quantum Compilers (QIR/Qiskit transpiler)

QIR carries quantum instructions on LLVM IR — quantum semantics parasitized on a classical IR. This paper's layer collapse (§11.1) gives the opposite direction: quantum gates and classical instructions are **different rows of the same operator table** (the IR-layer interpretation of the [Paper3] §9 mapping), and quantum compilation is not an independent discipline but another projection of the same compiler. This explains [Paper3]'s empirical fact: QFT₈ and NTT inter-translate exactly in GF$(65537)$ (see the round-trip verification of [Paper3] Appendix E).

### 23.6 Bootstrap History (C/Go/Rust bootstrapping)

The classical bootstrap of C compilers (assembly-written C → C-written C) corresponds to §6.1's object level; the modern bootstrap of Go/Rust (the language rewriting its compiler + seed chain) corresponds to textual self-hosting (§5.4's table). Both use **behavioral equivalence** as the criterion (compiled programs behave the same); this paper's physical bootstrap uses the **bit-level fixed point** (§7.2's strengthening), hence machine-decidable. Rust's bootstrap chain (mrustc seed) and this paper's seed build chain (QTN seed → self-compiling build) are engineering-isomorphic, but seed trust differs: Rust relies on community audit, this paper on triangular cross-check (§8).

### 23.7 Partial Evaluation and Supercompilation

Futamura projection (compiler specialization) and supercompilation (limit unfolding of program transformations) are the extreme of the "compilation = transformation" tradition. This paper's correspondence and advance: the Futamura first projection (specialized interpreter = compiler) takes the form here of "the residue field collapsing on itself" (§4 stage-pole self-reference); supercompilation's driving/generalize mechanism corresponds to the escape of the Julia terrain (§17). Difference: supercompilation's termination is undecidable (classical), this paper's is boundedly decidable (Theorem V3 — the finiteness of finite fields).

### 23.8 Proof-Producing Bootstrap (CakeML)

CakeML is a verified-compiler ecosystem for an ML subset: the compiler is verified in the HOL4 theorem prover, and **bootstrap completes inside the proof assistant** — when the compiler compiles itself, it produces machine code along with a functional-correctness theorem (proof-producing bootstrapping; Kumar et al. POPL 2014, Tan et al. JFP 2019, Myreen et al. ITP 2021). Comparison with this framework:

| Dimension | CakeML | This framework |
|---|---|---|
| correctness form | proof obligation (theorems produced with artifacts) | criterion obligation (bit-identical fixed-point comparison) |
| bootstrap semantics | compile self + theorem closure | three constraints (input self / output bit-identical / understand IR) |
| trust base | HOL4 proof kernel | six-operator axioms + triangular cross-check + fourth corner (§8) |
| language scale | ML subset (modules/FFI) | 12-keyword core subset |

**Relation**: CakeML makes correctness a **product of theorems** (compilation = proof), this framework makes correctness a **property of data** (IR = criterion). The former's expressiveness expands with proof engineering, the latter's criterion is intrinsic to the IR structure. The two are the two ends of the "verified compilation" spectrum, complementary rather than competitive; CakeML's proof-producing mechanism is the proof-layer dual of this framework's O20 (Futamura second projection).

### 23.9 Full-Source Bootstrap Chains (GNU Mes / live-bootstrap)

The bootstrappable-builds movement pursues rebuilding the complete toolchain from a minimal seed: GNU Mes starts from a 357-byte hex0 seed, amplifying level by level through Stage0 → M2-Planet → Mes (a Scheme interpreter + minimal C compiler) to full GNU/Linux (Guix integration, FOSDEM 2021; the 2025 main-line application is supply-chain trust — a remotely attestable boot chain). Comparison with this framework's bootstrap chain:

| Dimension | Mes chain | This framework |
|---|---|---|
| seed | 357B hex0 | ~600B launcher + ~5 syscalls (§6.5 physical boundary) |
| seed trust | shrink to human bit-auditable | triangular cross-check + four-corner representation verification (§8) |
| amplification | each level compiles a stronger compiler | seed AOT assembles the compiler once (§6.1) |
| end state | complete system, fully source-auditable | single-layered end state (2 atoms + full ΨLang layer) |

**Relation**: Mes's seed-trust strategy is "shrink to readable", this framework's is "cross-verify to trustworthy" — the former is friendly to human audit, the latter to machine verification. The two share one structural conclusion: **the trust anchor of a bootstrap system must lie outside the system** (human audit or mathematical criterion), consistent with the analysis of the Thompson attack (§8.1).

### 23.10 The Equality-Saturation Family (egg / ROVER / eqsat / DialEgg)

Equality saturation reconstructs the optimizer as "non-destructive rewriting + extraction", entering the industrial view in 2024–2025: ROVER optimizes RTL with verified e-graph rewriting (area up to −63%, IEEE TCAD 2024), eqsat nativizes equality saturation as an MLIR dialect (2025), DialEgg provides a dialect-agnostic optimizer (CGO 2025). This framework's correspondence and difference is detailed in §16.4 Proposition V37: rewrite rules come from the six-operator axioms rather than expert rule sets, legality is checked at extraction by the cross-ratio guard, and value-level termination is given by the boundedness of the finite-field value space. For first-hand industrial records, compare LambdaClass's effect.systems series (2025, engineering logs on MLIR/e-graph practice): its measured costs of effect systems and e-graph optimization (saturation scheduling, extraction overhead) provide fellow-traveler evidence for the implementation plan of O34.

### 23.11 RNS Homomorphic Compilation (RNS-CKKS/BFV)

RNS decomposition is the standard arithmetic base of practical homomorphic encryption (Bajard et al. SAC 2016; Cheon et al. ASIACRYPT 2017's CKKS and its RNS variants): the NTT-friendly prime admission of the modulus chain shares the number-theoretic criterion with this framework's prime pool; HE's rescaling depth management and DOI's domain-order ascent are opposite scale directions of the same CRT architecture (§18.4 Proposition V39). FHE compilers (compiling plaintext programs into homomorphic execution sequences on ciphertext) thereby become an unimplemented extension of this framework's "multi-backend" concept (O35).

### 23.12 ZX-calculus and Graph-Rewriting Quantum IRs

ZX-calculus constitutes a graphical calculus for quantum computation with spider diagrams + rewrite rules, used for circuit optimization (the PyZX line, Kissinger–van de Wetering, Quantum 2020) and extended to architecture search and databased rewriting (2025). Its relation to this framework is twofold: (i) **a quantum precedent of layer collapse** — ZX diagrams and quantum circuits are two labels of the same object, and the "circuit→ZX→simplify→extract" pipeline is of the same family, in spirit, as Theorem V6 (each layer = a projection of the same $\mathcal T$), and both are instances of "label layers are collapsible"; (ii) **the contrast of the extraction problem** — extracting a circuit from a ZX diagram is a known hard problem (non-planar-diagram extraction can explode exponentially), whereas the stack reconstruction of the residueon table is $O(m)$ — **the difference stems from the presence or absence of structural constraints**: ZX diagrams allow arbitrary graph topology (extraction must search for an order satisfying causal constraints), while the residueon table's post-order + arity encoding builds the topological constraints into the data (Theorem V1's injectivity comes exactly from this). This is further evidence that "reversibility requires IR structural constraints" (echoing Proposition V17 and §23.13).

### 23.13 Reversible Programming Languages (the Janus family) and Bidirectional Transformations

Janus (Lutz–Derby 1986; Yokoyama–Glück–Axelsen's self-inverse interpreter, PEPM 2012) imposes reversibility at the **source-language layer**: syntactic constraints (reversible assignments, conditions with exit assertions) guarantee stepwise reversibility. Lenses/bidirectional transformations impose reversibility on **view–model pairs**: get/put bifunctors satisfy round-trip laws. This framework's Kleene bijection imposes reversibility at the **IR layer**: emission/reconstruction are mutually inverse (Theorem V1). The three are orthogonal and composable: a reversible source language can compile to the residueon table while preserving the bijection; the residueon table as a serialization format is naturally a get/put pair of lenses (emit/get, rebuild/put, and the fourth corner's Python reader is an independent implementation of a read-only lens). The key difference from Janus: Janus's reversibility **constrains how programs are written** (expressiveness traded for reversibility), this framework's reversibility **constrains only the IR shape** (the source language is unrestricted) — at the cost that the IR must carry complete structural information (O18's trade-off problem).

### 23.14 Photonic Compilers and Photonic AI Acceleration (2025)

Photonic computing entered production in 2025 (detailed in §19 supplement three): Lightmatter's general photonic AI accelerator (Nature 2025, production workloads), Lightelligence's PACE photonic array, and compilation research for photonic-electronic hybrid systems (LightCode, 2025). Significance for §19: the physical background of the residueon-table→waveguide-parameter direct transcription is industrially established, but this framework's direct-transcription route still has no real-machine verification — related work updates the **background feasibility**, not this framework's verification status.

**How to read this section**. Among the fourteen comparisons, §23.1–§23.7 are **same-generation comparisons** — this framework shares the object "compilation" with those works, the difference being the design point; §23.8–§23.14 are **adjacent comparisons** — sharing a local mechanism (bootstrap, equality saturation, RNS, graph rewriting, reversibility, photonic backend) while the overall goals differ. Wherever the words "this framework is superior" appear in the comparison, their scope is limited to **the scale this framework actually reaches** (see §24.2's scale statement); on the three items of optimization depth, ecosystem scale, and target-platform count, LLVM, CompCert, and CakeML are not at the same magnitude as this framework — a fact left unadorned in this section.

### 24.1 Core Results

2. **Bootstrap layer** (Theorems V2/V3, Proposition V4): the rigorous definition of self-bootstrapping with three constraints (bit-identity at the core); the finite-field fixed point exists and terminates at the decision level; three tiers (object/meta/evolution) + the cap (no fourth order, the Gödel boundary).

- **O11 (the self-compilation fixed point $RT_2\equiv RT_3$)**: **the current highest priority**. ΨLang2's self-compilation test only verifies that "the self-compiler can generate $>100$ poles" (**can generate**), not that the generated $RT_2$ is **behaviorally equivalent** ($\equiv$) to the reference implementation's $RT_1$. Per Theorem V2's three constraints, "output bit-identical" is the core constraint — $RT_2\equiv RT_3$ (or $RT_2\equiv RT_1$) is the completion state of bootstrap. Without this verification, the statement "self-compilation succeeded" **does not hold**: it only shows $RT_2$ exists, not that $RT_2$ is a correct compiler. The V3 implementation achieves this via the pole-by-pole assertions of V11-043's 166 poles and V11-045's 420 poles; the ΨLang2 implementation is pending.
- **O12 (cross-implementation artifact inter-conversion)**: V3 and ΨLang2 residueon tables are **format-isomorphic** with dual field-role assignments (§3.1 note), with no converter between them. If built, Proposition V5's triangular verification gains a **second cross-implementation triangle** (V3 three-channel × ΨLang2 three-channel), greatly strengthening independence. Obstacles: different field-semantics conventions ($z$ vs $n$ as opcode), different opcode spaces (22 vs 127), different domain support (V3 dual-domain + prime pool vs ΨLang2 main GF(65537)).
- **O13 (fourth-corner automation)**: §8.5's Python independent reader is not connected to an automated audit chain and is currently invoked manually. After integration, each release automatically generates a "four-corner consistency" certificate.
- **O14 (AMM root extraction)**: the split branch of [Paper4] Corollary 22.4 for general $n$ needs the Adleman–Manders–Miller algorithm. **Advance (§14.6 layer 1)**: AMM is implemented and verified — integrated on the Rust verification-engine side (with deterministic non-residue search, V13.5 stage), and the ΨLang-side root-extraction routine is in place (the radical-evaluation module of the math library). Remaining work: connect that routine into the split-branch dispatch of AutoGear routing (the routing still conservatively returns 0), and make the gear corpus runnable under the canonical syntax (O41). This is the last piece of AutoGear (D.5) from "solvability-aware field selection" to "full-radical solvability".
- **O15 (static determination of order-dependence)**: Proposition V13 (three-channel certificate) requires order-independence of the program, currently observable only post-run (after-the-fact). How to statically determine that premise holds is unresolved.
- **O16 (mechanization of determinism guarantees)**: the iteration order of a hash set is process-random in the host language — automatic import once produced irreproducible results because of it (fix: sort before loading). Such "**non-nondeterminism leaking from the data structure**" problems need systematic screening and guards (e.g. a lint rule forbidding direct iteration of hash containers on compilation paths). This is a requirement of the three-no principle (no randomness) at the **implementation layer** (not the semantic layer).
- **O17 (the loop extension of BTA)**: the compile-time evaluator judges a whole function dynamic when its body contains loops or conditionals, so static subexpressions inside loop bodies are not specialized. This is the current **main capability gap** of partial evaluation. The standard repair path: loop unrolling to a static bound + multi-valued-binding BTA (each variable carrying a two-valued "static value ∨ dynamic" annotation, instead of the current whole-function two-valued). After the fix, Proposition V18's monad structure is unchanged, only its base type extended.
- **O18 (the trade-off rules between reversibility and size optimization)**: Proposition V17 requires the IR to keep complete structural information to maintain the bijection. This conflicts with certain size optimizations: dead-code elimination breaks $\mathrm{rebuild}\circ\mathrm{emit}=\mathrm{id}$ (the rebuilt tree differs from the original), as does dropping type information. A **clear trade-off rule** is needed — which optimizations may break the bijection and how to annotate after the break (e.g. mark the optimized artifact "non-rebuildable" and route it through a separate verification path). The current implementation does no such optimization, so the problem has not appeared; it must be adjudicated once an optimization channel is introduced. This is a downstream problem that the "IR as artifact" paradigm choice necessarily encounters.
- **O19 (the audit of backporting theoretical corrections to the implementation)**: [Paper4] proves the (Op) rule should be $\mathrm{lcm}(k_1,k_2)$, not $\max(k_1,k_2)$ (minimal counterexample $\deg(a+b)=6=\mathrm{lcm}(2,3)$), but the engineering side's domain-order-inference module still implements the $\max$ version (its merge-function comment literally says "merging two domains: take $\max(k_1,k_2)$ — the (Op) rule"), and the module is marked "unused code allowed" in several places, not connected to the main compilation flow. **This is a real risk**: an unconnected module carrying a rule falsified by theory would introduce a silent error if ever enabled. Two dispositions: backport the correction, or retire the orphan module per Series V's residual criterion. The same audit should extend to other orphan-marked modules.

This framework's specializer can currently specialize only **pure arithmetic expressions**, not itself (it contains `match`, recursion, a hash map), so the second projection is **currently unreachable**. Worth studying: whether self-applicability is easier under the residueon-table representation than the syntax-tree representation (because the residueon table itself is data and $\mathrm{rebuild}$ is reversible) — if so, "a specializer written as a residueon table specializing a specializer written as a residueon table" is a concrete path to a compiler generator. ## Appendix D: Engineering Supplements

### D.1 Proposition V10: ALU Containerization — the Separation of ISA and Implementation

**Definition** (ALU-ISA): the operator-source instruction set, together with its execution contract (stack effects, domain-switching protocol, termination semantics), constitutes an abstract-machine specification. **Container** = any implementation of that specification.

**Proposition V10**: if a container $C$ is bit-identical (same domain value) to the reference implementation $R$ on all test programs, then for any residueon table $T$, the credibility of $C(T)\equiv R(T)$ is monotonically bounded by test coverage — and where bit-identity breaks, it **precisely localizes** the representational difference of the two implementations (see D.6).

**Architectural corollary**: the backend taxonomy is corrected from "backend = target language" to "ALU = ISA, container = implementation": {reference container, X86-ALU (AOT), WASM-ALU (portable), GPU-ALU (SIMT)}; the tree executor is the **semantic oracle**, not a member of the ALU family (it consumes not the ISA but the AST, the semantic preimage of the ISA). WASM is reclassified from "a peer backend" to a portable container of the ALU — the correction of the taxonomy is itself this round's result.

**Evidence**: the reference container already supports direct loading of operator source; X86-ALU and GPU-ALU are the follow-up roadmap (deterministic machine-code encoding → fingerprintable, see D.8).

### D.2 Proposition V11: Link Uniqueness — Label Collisions in a Purely Numerical ISA

**Proposition V11**: if modules $M_1,\dots,M_k$ each independently emit a label set $\{L_0,\dots,L_{n_i}\}$ and are concatenated into one instruction stream with unified backpatching, then when $k>1$ and control flow exists, the jump-misalignment probability is 1 (the label table's later writes overwrite earlier ones ⟹ all non-final modules' labels resolve to the same-name label addresses of the final module).

**Fix** (single-pass merged emission): merge the module ASTs and emit once; labels are naturally globally unique.

**Significance**: this is the recurrence, in a **purely numerical ISA**, of the same lesson of C static linking (symbol-name conflicts) and WASM (index spaces) — the symbol-management problem does not vanish because the representation layer "only has numbers": **hash = value identity (collidable, $1/65537$), label = control-flow identity (non-collidable)**. Which layer of representation needs uniqueness is decided by the semantic role of that layer.

**Evidence**: V13.1 corpus baseline 55/208 → 210/210 after the label fix; an 18k-line corpus execution dropped from 20 s of runaway allocation to 33 ms, dual-engine bit-identical.

### D.3 Two Inverses and the Closure/Split Duality (the radical-semantics erratum, the engineering-side record of main-text Proposition V20)

**Proposition** (radical semantics): over GF$(p)$, the correct execution of $a^{1/n}$ needs the **exponent-ring inverse** $n^{-1}\bmod(p{-}1)$, not the field inverse $n^{-1}\bmod p$. The two are unequal for general $p$.
- When $\gcd(n,p{-}1)=1$: $x\mapsto x^n$ is bijective, and the unique root $a^{n^{-1}\bmod(p-1)}$ is exact (**closed**);
- When $\gcd(n,p{-}1)>1$: **the shell opens** (multiple roots); for $n{=}2$ and $p\equiv3\pmod4$ the quadratic formula $a^{(p+1)/4}$ is exact ($\mathrm{Legendre}(a)=1$); the general case needs AMM (O14).

**Closure/split duality**: the closure condition $\gcd(n,p{-}1)=1$ and the split condition $n\mid(p{-}1)$ point in opposite directions — in the prime pool, the Fermat poles ($p{-}1=2^{16}$, closing all odd radicals) and the Mersenne-type poles ($131071$, split-rich) form a **complementary radical-pole pair**. This is the second proof of the prime-pool admission criterion (the first being the NTT $v_2$ division of labor). The same literal $1/n$ takes the field inverse in division context and the ring inverse in radical context — **context-sensitive semantics**.

**Evidence**: $\sqrt[3]{8}=2$, $\sqrt[3]{2}=2048$, $\sqrt[5]{32}=2$; $\sqrt{2}=512$ @GF(131071) ($512^2\equiv2$, routing report [autogear]).

### D.7 The Numerical Layer: the Field-Selection Interface, the Reconstruction Word Width, and One-Time Boundary Scaling

**The field-selection interface** (the implementation side of [Paper4] §7.2). Three composable interfaces:

| Interface | Constraint | Semantics | Measured routing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| select by order | $k_j\mid p-1$ | algebraic demand ($\sqrt[3]{a}$ needs $3\mid p{-}1$) | $k{=}3/5/17\to131071$; $k{=}2,4,\dots,256\to65537$; $k{=}2^{18}\to786433$ |
| select by value range | $p>2M$ | value-range demand (against finite-field **silent wraparound**) | $M{\le}1000\to65537$; ${\le}40000\to131071$; ${\le}80000\to786433$ |
| joint selection | both | value range first on conflict | $(k{=}3,M{=}200000)\to786433$ |

*Rule*: the priority order is not a heuristic — violating the value range causes **silent wraparound** (result wrong but formally legal), violating the order only causes "cannot compute" (apparently no solution, fixable by raising the field). Hence value range wins on conflict, consistent with the "danger level: high" row of [Paper4] Corollary 22.2's criterion table.

**The reconstruction word width** (the implementation side of [Paper4] Corollary 7.4). The four-prime-pool product $\prod p_i=9\,297\,248\,521\,260\,198\,002\,687>2^{72}$ far exceeds the 64-bit signed-integer bound $2^{63}{-}1=9\,223\,372\,036\,854\,775\,807$, so the **reconstruction step** of CRT (Garner / continued fractions) must use 128-bit integers; whereas the **arithmetic of the remainder components themselves** (per-component modular arithmetic) fits 64 bits entirely when $p_i<2^{21}$. This separation confines the cost of word-width expansion to $O(k)$ reconstruction operations, not the bulk arithmetic.

*One fixed implementation erratum*. An early implementation misused 64-bit integers in the reconstruction step, producing silent overflow. Its classification is worth recording: it belongs to the **value-range-constraint** category, not the **order-constraint** category, of [Paper4] Corollary 22.2 — manifesting as "computed wrong" rather than "cannot compute". The three-channel certificate (Proposition V13) is precisely the runtime defense for that category.

**The implementation of modular reduction**. [Paper4] §2.1(iii) argues the **modular-reduction friendliness** of the two primes (structural reasons); its implementation form is pure bit operations:

```
// Fermat reduction GF(65537): ab = q·2^16 + r  ⟹  ab ≡ r − q  (since 2^16 ≡ −1)
p = a*b;  r = p & 0xFFFF;  q = p >> 16;  d = r − q;
d >= 0 ? d : d + 65537
// Mersenne reduction GF(131071 = 2^17 − 1):  (x & M) + (x >> 17), loop folding
```

After reduction, $a,b<2^{16}{+}1$ makes the 64-bit product $<2^{33}$ never overflow — **zero division, zero wide integers**. Similarly, inversion is changed from Fermat's little theorem $a^{65535}$ (32 multiplications) to the extended Euclidean algorithm (about 16 iterations), mathematically equivalent, 2× performance.

*Reduction is a replaceable implementation detail*: it changes no mathematical value, hence enters no theorem chain of [Paper4]. The optimization is back-ported from the engineering implementation to V3, and **bit-identity is unchanged** after the port — exactly the evidence of "implementations are replaceable".

**Q64.64 and exact rationals: scaling happens only once at the boundary**. An exact real is a rational pair $(n,d)$, each represented as a CRT residue vector, so **all internal operations are exactly precise** (addition/subtraction/multiplication/division are all integer operations; division takes the modular inverse). Only when **crossing the external boundary** (outputting a decimal string, or handing to an external consumer) is it converted once into fixed-point:

$$\text{internal: exact}\ \frac{n}{d}\ \xrightarrow[\text{exactly once}]{\text{boundary}}\ \text{Q64.64:}\left\lfloor\frac{n\cdot2^{64}}{d}\right\rceil$$

*Discipline meaning*. A classical floating-point pipeline introduces and accumulates error **at every step** ($0.1\times10\neq1$); this pipeline introduces scaling at **exactly one place** ($10\times\frac{1}{10}=1$, exact throughout). This is the realization of the three-no principle at the **numerical layer** (not the arithmetic layer): no-float is not the slogan "don't use floating-point numbers" but the architectural property "**error has a single entry point**". The decimal-output conversion is for display only and takes no part in any computation path.

### D.9 Negative-Encoding Strings: Using the Value Domain's Negative Space for References, Zero Added Syntax [VERIFIED]

**Proposition V16 (negative-encoding handle)**. GF$(p)$'s value range is $[0,p-1]$, and the **negative space is entirely idle**. A string can therefore be encoded as the **negative handle of a heap slot**:

$$\mathrm{hdl}(s)=-\,(\mathrm{slot}(s)+1)$$

where $\mathrm{slot}(s)$ is the heap slot of the character-code array of string $s$. The $+1$ guarantees the handle of slot $0$ is $-1\neq0$ ($0$ is reserved for "empty/false").

*Why this is a key design*. The framework's largest known limitation is "strings degenerate to hashes, losing content". Negative encoding repairs it without **adding a keyword, changing the residueon-table format, or adding an IR type** — the repair happens entirely in the **interpretation of values**:

| Semantics | Implementation | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| equality / inequality | content comparison (an engine-front check, not handle comparison) | handle comparison gives wrong results |
| concatenation | digitize the non-string side, then concatenate segment by segment | a dedicated concatenation instruction |
| length / character access | check the length attribute first, then magic-number scan | |
| interpolation | **true rendering**: literal segments make strings, expression segments convert to strings, segment-by-segment concatenation, left-folded | previously hash concatenation |
| ℤ-domain degradation | strings degrade to ordinary arrays | because ℤ has no idle negative space |

**The bit-identity trap (the first version overturned right here)**. The tree executor's **allocation sequence** must **strictly mirror** the virtual machine's emission: literal segments make strings, expression segments convert to strings, segment-by-segment concatenation, left-folded. Any order difference causes handle-number drift (measured ALU $=-14$ vs tree executor $=-7$) — **bit-identity captures the allocation order, not the final string value**. This is the second instance of Proposition V14 (bit-identity = divergence localizer).

**Relation to the type system**. "Type tag = arithmetic interval": negative = reference, non-negative = domain value; structural values (objects/ranges) are distinguished by heap-head magic numbers. **Zero boxing, zero tagged union** — type checking degenerates into one interval comparison. Traps and rules: **value identity is collidable** (GF hash, $1/65537$), **reference identity is non-collidable** (handle); range endpoints belong to ℤ (loop indices are not field elements).

### D.11 WASM Emission: Zero-Dependency Hand Coding and the Three-End Verification Loop [VERIFIED]

Backend emission depends on no third-party encoder — ULEB128/SLEB128 encodings are **entirely hand-written**, the four sections (type / function / export / code) are constructed byte by byte, with content being GF helper functions (add, subtract, multiply, Clifford, Hebbian, Born) + user functions + the main-function export.

**The three-end verification loop**:

1. **Structural check** — magic number, version, section layout, function-body closure;
2. **An independent engine's module validator** — a true validator (not self-certifying) ✓;
3. **Instantiation and execution** — $3*5+7\to\mathrm{main}()=22$, GF semantics correct.

*One typical error*. The signed-less-than instruction of the subtraction helper **lacks the comparison's left operand**: WASM has no shorthand for "compare with 0", requiring an explicit push of constant 0. The signature of this class of error is **passing the structural check but failing the validator** — the value of the three-end loop lies exactly here: single-end checking (especially a self-implemented structural check) misses the entire "instruction operand-stack-type" class of errors.

*Relation to Proposition V9*. This is a concrete instance of multi-backend homogeneity: the same residueon table → WASM, and that WASM is verifiable and executable by an **independent implementation outside this framework** (the V8 engine). Not a proof of Proposition V9, but runnable evidence for it on the WASM backend.

### D.12 Determinism Guarantees: the Leak of Non-Nondeterminism from Data Structures [VERIFIED, fixed]

The "no randomness" of the three-no principle is guaranteed at the **semantic layer** by "the random interface always returns zero". But one measured incident shows that **implementation-layer** non-nondeterminism can bypass that guarantee:

**Incident**. Automatic import pulls candidate modules by the missing call name from the standard-library index (3997 functions), deduplicates with a hash set, and iterates to load. The iteration order of a hash set is **process-random** in the host language (randomized hash seed), so different runs load modules in **different orders**; when multiple modules define the same name, **the later loader overwrites the earlier** ⟹ the result is **irreproducible**.

**Fix**. Sort before iteration. Cost $O(k\log k)$, in exchange for reproducibility.

*Lesson*. "No randomness" cannot be guaranteed only by the semantic layer (the random interface) — **any code path depending on set or hash iteration order is a potential source of non-nondeterminism**. This suggests a lint rule (O16): **compilation paths must not iterate hash containers directly; sort before iterating**. This is an instance of this framework pushing "determinism" from **axiom** to **code norm**: the three-no principle is not a slogan but a set of mechanically checkable constraints.

**The list of same-family problems** (all "non-nondeterminism leaking from the implementation layer"): ① hash-set iteration order (fixed); ② the reduction order of parallel iteration (this framework uses no parallelism); ③ floating-point operation order (nonexistent, no float); ④ addresses as hash keys (unused). Three of the four are naturally excluded by the structural choices of "no float + no parallelism + no pointer hashing" — **the constraints of the axiom layer keep yielding dividends at the implementation layer**.

*The claim it verifies*. This is not "translating algorithms" but verifying a falsifiable proposition: **any finite-precision algorithm can land losslessly on the pure-integer domain of GF$(p)$**. Including algorithms traditionally dependent on floating-point semantics, such as backpropagation, GAN, and MCMC. If the claim holds, **determinism, reproducibility, and hardware-izability** arrive together ([Paper4] Corollary 8.4's spectral-domain learning is its theorization on learning algorithms).

*Boundaries and limitations*. ① The list is an **existence proof** (every algorithm is implementable on the field), not a **performance argument** (field implementations need not be faster — e.g. sorting-class algorithms are naturally inapplicable because the field is unordered). ② "Lossless" means **arithmetically lossless** (no rounding), not **semantically equivalent** (K-means over a finite field and K-means over the reals give different clusterings, because the distance measure differs). ③ Some implementations are bit-width limited (a single modular-multiply result is $<2^{17}$), and long-chain computation needs CRT or mosaic boundary extension.
