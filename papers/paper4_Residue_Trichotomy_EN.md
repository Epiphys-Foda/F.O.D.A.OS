## 3 The Interpolation Theorem: Non-Differential Generation of Nested Fractals

### 3.1 Three nesting-generation mechanisms

**Mechanism M1 (self-similar iterative generation)**. Infinite nesting levels are produced directly by Möbius self-maps / generating rules. The poles of a meromorphic function naturally nest self-similar Fatou/Julia structures — direct products of iterative dynamics.

**Mechanism M2 (SRRF self-referential fixed-point generation)**. A non-trivial fixed point $\Psi^*$ of the SRRF equation is itself an infinitely nested residue field — the poles on the right side sit exactly at $\Psi(w_K)$, while $\Psi(w_K)$ is in turn defined by deeper sub-poles. **Key point**: this nesting persists even where the classical pole description of a simple pole fails (non-analytic, Planck-truncated) — the self-referential loop closes, and nesting emerges from within the algebraic constraint.

**Mechanism M3 (collision-emergent generation)**. $\text{Res}_A\star\text{Res}_B\to\text{new liusu}_C$ generates new poles and new levels at run time (a residueon dynamic hypergraph), driven by algebraic collision rules.

**The nesting-release operator (replacing differentiation)**. The classical notation $\frac{d^n}{dz^n}[r_0/(z-z_0)]$ has, in this framework, the status of a **nesting-release operator**: it releases, layer by layer, the sub-field already compressed inside a singularity to the outside — a lossless unfolding, not a "creation". Nesting generation is the primary operator throughout; differentiation appears only as a comparative alias in the smooth limit and participates in no generating mechanism.

### 3.2 The Interpolation Theorem

**Theorem 1 (exactness of interpolation — non-differential version)**. Let a nested fractal be generated by any of (M1)–(M3), with the internal field strictly isomorphic to the parent field. Then the internal nested expansion at any level $n$ is **analytically exact**, with zero external parameters and zero truncation error.

_Proof_. Given independently for the three mechanisms.

**(M1) Iterative case**. The generating map $f$ and the scale transform $h$ satisfy the conjugacy $\Psi^{(n+1)}=h\circ\Psi^{(n)}\circ h^{-1}$. By induction, $\Psi^{(n)}=h^n\circ\Psi^{(0)}\circ h^{-n}$ — the $n$-th level is an exact conjugate image of level 0, strictly isomorphic in structure and **containing no approximation step**. The self-similar fixed point $\Psi^{(*)}$ satisfies $h\circ\Psi^{(*)}\circ h^{-1}=\Psi^{(*)}$, at which nesting is level-independent. For simple poles: the Laurent coefficients of the differential unfolding
$$\frac{d^n}{dz^n}\left[\frac{r_0}{z-z_0}\right]=\frac{(-1)^n\,n!\,r_0}{(z-z_0)^{n+1}}$$
give $a_{-(n+1)}=(-1)^n n!\,r_0$ — this is a **corollary** of the child-field structure under iteration of $f$ (induction on $n$ from the conjugacy yields the same coefficient sequence), not a precondition of the fractal's existence. Viewing the interior (differentiation) and generating the interior (iteration) commute in this case, but the latter does not depend on the former.

**(M2) Self-referential case**. The SRRF fixed point $\Psi^*$ is defined by algebraic constraints (the polynomial system of §5.5); its poles lie in the image of $\Psi$, and each pole's residue is a value of $\Psi'$ — nesting is a **solution of constraints**, not a limit of iterations; exactness comes from the closure of polynomial solutions (finiteness of roots over GF(p), Berlekamp-decomposable). Whether a simple pole is "classically describable" never enters the constraints — under non-analytic singularities or Planck truncation the system remains well-posed.

**(M3) Collision case**. The collision rule $\star$ is an algebraic rewrite: each collision's new pole/residue is given exactly by the rule table; the growth of infinite levels is the rewrite sequence — nesting depth = rewrite steps, each of zero error.

**Common conclusion of the three mechanisms**: every step of the nested expansion is a closed-form exact integer/analytic object; there is no "approximate after truncation" step — zero truncation error is not "very small" in the approximate sense but structurally non-existent. □

**Parameter count**. A multi-pole difference of order $n$ needs $2M$ parameters ($M\sim n$); (M1) needs only $f$ (constant many) $+\ n$; (M2) only a seed; (M3) only the collision rules — none grow linearly with $n$. This is a dimension-level gap in parameter efficiency (§6.1).

### 3.6 The algebraic-geometric structure of the residue field: entanglement, error correction, and spectral sequences

The residue field is not only an evaluator (§3.4) and a syntactic carrier (§3.5); its configuration space itself carries algebraic-geometric structure. This section states four propositions that unify "entanglement", "error correction", "complexity", and "fault tolerance" as geometric properties of the configuration space. **Confidence is graded item by item and must not be read uniformly**: Propositions 3.6a–3.6c are **structural proposals** (PROPOSAL/HYPOTHESIS) — the definitions and constructions are exact over GF$(p)$, but complete formal verification has not been executed; Proposition 3.6d is **verified** (VERIFIED) — a standard consequence of the residue theorem, corroborated numerically, and therefore not to be treated on a par with a–c.

**Proposition 3.6a (entanglement = Grassmannian rank condition)** [PROPOSAL]. An $n$-qubit state is a vector $v\in GF(p)^{2^n}$. The separable states ($v=w_1\otimes w_2$) form the image of the Segre embedding — a low-dimensional subvariety; a general state corresponds to a point of the projective space $\mathbb P(GF(p)^{2^n})$, whose entanglement is characterized by the rank of the bipartition coefficient matrix $C$:
$$v\ \text{separable}\iff \mathrm{rank}(C)=1\iff \text{all }2\times2\text{ minors (Plücker coordinates) vanish}$$
Entangled states are the points whose Plücker coordinates do not all vanish (the complement-of-Grassmannian $G(1,2^n)$ structure). Hence: **entanglement degree = the algebraic value of the Plücker coordinates**; entanglement classification = rank stratification; a Bell measurement in this language is **residue extraction** — projection onto a specified Plücker coordinate followed by normalization. This shares a source with [Paper3]'s conclusion that the zero-norm state $(1,i)/\sqrt2$ must live in GF$(p^2)$: every determinant needed by the rank condition is already a field element; entanglement needs no Hilbert-space structure, only linear algebra over a field.

**Proposition 3.6b (quantum error correction = cohomology)** [PROPOSAL]. A quantum error-correcting code can be formulated as a linear subspace over the algebraic variety $V$ in the Grassmannian $G(k,2^n)$: poles carry physical qubits (carriers), residues carry logical qubits (information), and the differential ($z$-shift operator; the historical notation $\Psi^{\natural}$) corresponds to syndrome measurement. Correction capability is given by the dimensions of the cohomology groups $H^i(V,\mathbb Z)$. This generalizes the cross-ratio-as-syndrome / Möbius-as-corrector scheme at the end of §3.5 (T10): syndromes are sections of $H^0$, and the group action of correctors preserves cohomology classes.

**Conjecture 3.6c (spectral-sequence collapse)** [HYPOTHESIS]. The configuration filtration of an $n$-qubit system (stratified by pole count) induces a spectral sequence that collapses on the $r=n$ page; this yields a **cohomological lower bound** on quantum algorithmic complexity: complexity $\ge$ the number of non-zero cohomology classes of the differential map. If true, lower-bound arguments for search problems acquire an algebraic-topological form; its continuum version connects with T4 (the adelic limit).

**Proposition 3.6d (residue topological fault tolerance — a perturbation invariant gives fault tolerance without error-correcting codes)** [VERIFIED]. Let $\psi(z)=\sum_k r_k/(z-z_k)^{n_k}$ be a residue field whose pole positions are perturbed as $z_k\mapsto z_k+\delta_k$ ($\delta_k\neq0$, with the perturbed poles remaining distinct and distinct from the evaluation point). Then
$$\forall k:\quad \mathrm{Res}_{z=z_k+\delta_k}\big[\psi_{\text{perturbed}}\big]=r_k ,$$
i.e. **the residue is strictly invariant under perturbation of the pole position**. Since the result of a computation is defined as residue extraction (the grand unification equation of Theorems 12/13: the $n$-th term = residue extraction), **the result is immune to arbitrary perturbation of pole positions**.

_Proof_. The residue is a local quantity: for a simple pole $z_k$, $\mathrm{Res}_{z_k}\psi=\lim_{z\to z_k}(z-z_k)\psi(z)=r_k$, a limit depending only on the principal-part coefficient of the Laurent expansion of $\psi$ in a punctured neighbourhood of $z_k$, and not on the **absolute position** of $z_k$. Perturbing $z_k\mapsto z_k+\delta_k$ translates the entire Laurent expansion, leaving the principal-part coefficient $r_k$ unchanged. Higher-order poles ($n_k>1$) are the same: the Laurent coefficient $a_{-m}$ is invariant under translation. □

**Corollary (topological fault tolerance versus Shannon-style error correction)**. Classical fault tolerance follows the Shannon route: **add redundancy** (an error-correcting code) → detect → correct, at the cost of a rate loss $R<1$ and encoding/decoding latency. The residue-field route is orthogonal: **the information sits in a topological invariant** (residue = contour integral = homology class), so perturbation moves the location of a singularity without changing its residue, and **no redundancy, no detection, and no correction is needed**.

| | Shannon-style error correction (ECC) | residue topological fault tolerance (this proposition) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| object protected | the **codeword** after encoding (redundant bits) | **the residue itself** (the semantic carrier) |
| mechanism | distance separation + decoding | topological invariance |
| cost | rate loss, codec latency | zero (invariance is free) |
| scope | error patterns within the designed distance | perturbation of **pole position** (**not** of residue value) |

_Honest boundary (essential)_. This proposition protects **only** pole positions, **not** residue values: perturbing $r_k\mapsto r_k+\varepsilon$ changes the result directly. It is therefore not a replacement for ECC but an **orthogonal layer** — applicable to physical carriers in which device placement is imprecise while coupling strengths are controllable (drift of waveguide coordinates on a photonic chip, component tolerances in analog computation), which is precisely the dominant error source in the hardware mapping of §19. ### 5.5 Explicit dynamics of the SRRF bootstrap and an honest negative result

To keep Theorem 3 from remaining an existence statement, this section gives the explicit SRRF map and one systematic computational experiment.

**Explicit two-pole SRRF map**. Take pre-poles $w_1,w_2$ and the pole/residue state $(Z_1,Z_2,R_1,R_2)$; the field and its derivative are
$$\Psi(z)=\frac{R_1}{z-Z_1}+\frac{R_2}{z-Z_2},\qquad \Psi'(z)=-\frac{R_1}{(z-Z_1)^2}-\frac{R_2}{(z-Z_2)^2}$$
The SRRF constraints $Z_k=\Psi(w_k),\ R_k=\Psi'(w_k)$ define the **bootstrap map** on state space
$$T:\ (Z_1,Z_2,R_1,R_2)\ \mapsto\ \big(\Psi(w_1),\Psi(w_2),\Psi'(w_1),\Psi'(w_2)\big)$$
whose fixed points are the SRRF self-consistent solutions; the pole and residue spectra are fully determined by $(w_1,w_2)$ and the algebraic structure — the finite-dimensional instance of Theorem 3.

**Computational experiment (honest negative result)**. Over GF(65537) with $w_1=3,w_2=7$, all $38\times29\times25=27{,}550$ seeds in the box $Z_1\in[2,40), Z_2\in[41,70), R_1,R_2\in[1,6)$ were iterated 60 steps (singular orbits hitting poles skipped): **none converged to a fixed point** — discrete orbits over GF(p) enter long cycles rather than converging.

**Analysis (why it is hard, and the right solution)**. Over GF(p) there is no notion of "distance shrinking"; the classical convergence mechanism of fixed-point iteration (contraction) does not apply. The fixed points are the solutions of a four-variable polynomial system:

### 7.1 The prime pool: a growable realization of Theorem 4

Theorem 4 demotes precision to the engineering parameter "number of primes", but does not answer **which** primes may enter the pool, nor what the **admission criterion** is. This section supplies that link — not new mathematics, but the operatorization of Theorem 4 and Theorem 22 (the order-matching law) in pool management.

**Theorem 30 (prime-pool admission and the exclusion of negative assets)**. Let $\mathcal P=\{p_1,\dots,p_k\}$ be a pool. A candidate prime $q$ may be admitted if and only if
$$\text{admit}(q)\iff \underbrace{\gcd(q,p_i)=1\ \forall i}_{\text{CRT precondition (Theorem 4)}}\ \wedge\ \underbrace{\Big(v_2(q-1)\ge 16\ \vee\ \#\{\ell\ \text{odd prime}:\ \ell\mid q-1\}\ge 3\Big)}_{\text{non-negative-asset condition}}$$
where the second clause is the **non-negative-asset condition**: it requires $q$ to make a net contribution in at least one of the two dimensions "transform length" (large $v_2$ $\Rightarrow$ long NTTs) and "radical splitting" (many odd factors $\Rightarrow$ multi-valued radicals, higher reciprocity laws).

_Proof_. The first clause is the precondition of the ring isomorphism of Theorem 4 (coprimality is necessary and sufficient for CRT). For the second: the **merged freedom** of the pool is governed by the factor structure of $\mathrm{lcm}_i(p_i-1)$ (Theorem 22). A new member that can supply neither 2-power factors ($v_2<16$, uselessly lowering $\min_i v_2$) nor sufficiently many odd factors ($<3$) only increases the reconstruction cost of CRT (a larger product $\Rightarrow$ a wider representation) without adding any solvability — it is a **negative asset**. □

_Honest boundary_. The thresholds $16$ and $3$ are **engineering thresholds, not mathematical constants**: 16 comes from $v_2(65537-1)=16$ in the Fermat field (already sufficient for every NTT length demanded in practice), and 3 from "accommodate at least $\zeta_3$ and $\zeta_5$, plus one spare". Changing the size distribution of the workload changes the optimal thresholds; the **form** of the criterion (a disjunction over non-negative assets) is invariant, the thresholds are calibration parameters.

**Measured (machine-verified)**. The candidates $12289=3\cdot2^{12}+1$ ($v_2=12<16$, odd part $\{3\}$) and $40961=5\cdot2^{13}+1$ ($v_2=13<16$, odd part $\{5\}$) are both refused admission; the four incumbent members ($65537/131071/786433/1376257$) all pass the audit.

### 7.2 The selection criterion and its priority order: the decidable form of Corollary 22.2

Corollary 22.2 decomposes field selection into two independent criteria, an **order constraint** and a **value-range constraint**. On a pool $\mathcal P$, their conjunction yields the selection map
$$\text{select}\big(\underbrace{\{k_j\}}_{\text{order}},\ \underbrace{M=\max|e|}_{\text{range}}\big)=\begin{cases} \min\{p\in\mathcal P:\ k_j\mid p-1\ \forall j\ \wedge\ p>2M\}, & \text{if it exists}\\ \min\{p\in\mathcal P:\ p>2M\}, & \text{otherwise (**range first**)}\\ \text{no solution (apparent unsolvability, Theorem 22)}, & \text{if the range is also unsatisfiable} \end{cases}$$
Its **priority order** is not a heuristic but a direct consequence of a difference in **detectability** between the two failure modes: violating the range constraint causes **silent wrap-around** (a wrong result that is formally legal — high severity), whereas violating the order constraint merely causes "cannot compute" (apparent unsolvability, correctable by ascending — low severity). Hence range takes priority on conflict — verbatim consistent with the criterion table of Corollary 22.2. The implementation of this map (three composable interfaces and their measured routing) is given in [Paper5]; this section records only its mathematical content.

### 7.3 The run-time cost and the run-time proof of value-range constraints

Corollary 22.2 gives a **static** criterion for value-range constraints ($p>2\max|e|$, requiring compile-time range analysis, which in practice is often unavailable). This section treats two questions: first, the word-width cost of value-range constraints under **multi-prime reconstruction** (Corollary 7.4); second, the **run-time proof** of value-range constraints — replacing range analysis by mutual attestation of three channels (Proposition 7.3).

**Proposition 7.3 (three-channel certificate)**. Let $P$ be a program without **order dependence** (its result does not depend on evaluation order — GF$(p)$ carries no natural order; see [Paper3], truth three), and let the true value $t$ satisfy $|t|<P_{12}/2$ where $P_{12}=p_1p_2$ for two coprime pool primes. Executing $P$ in three channels — GF$(p_1)$, GF$(p_2)$, and $\mathbb Z$ — yields $v_1,v_2,v_{\mathbb Z}$, and then
$$\mathrm{CRT}(v_1,v_2)=t=v_{\mathbb Z}\iff P\text{'s value-range constraint }\rho\text{ is unviolated in all three channels}$$

_Proof_. $(\Rightarrow)$ If $\rho$ is unviolated, then $t$ is uniquely determined within $[-P_{12}/2,P_{12}/2)$ by the residue pair $(v_1,v_2)$ (Theorem 4), so $\mathrm{CRT}(v_1,v_2)=t$; and the $\mathbb Z$ channel, having no reduction, yields $t$ directly. $(\Leftarrow)$ If $\rho$ is violated in the GF$(p_i)$ channel, that channel wraps around, so $v_i\neq t\bmod p_i$ and the CRT reconstruction deviates from $t$; if $\rho$ is violated in the $\mathbb Z$ channel (64-bit overflow), then $v_{\mathbb Z}\neq t$. Either way the equality fails. □

**Divergence is a signal**. Three-channel disagreement is not a failure but a **detector** for three classes of defect: ① order dependence (the GF channels have no natural order while the $\mathbb Z$ channel does, so disagreement reveals that the program used order); ② value-range overflow (wrap-around); ③ multi-valued radicals (the GF channel took a different branch from $\mathbb Z$). This converts [Paper3]'s truth three ("finite fields are unordered") from a limitation into a diagnostic instrument.

**Measured**. Take $t=12345\cdot6789+777=83810982$ (27 bits). The three channels give $v_1=54696$ (GF 65537), $v_2=56613$ (GF 131071), $v_{\mathbb Z}=83810982$; CRT reconstruction of $v_1,v_2$ gives $83810982=v_{\mathbb Z}$ ✓. **Two 17-bit residues recover a 27-bit true value** — small fields computing large numbers, the certificate being the proof.

_Relation to verifiable computation_. This proposition runs parallel to elliptic-curve-based verifiable computation, but uses **pure-integer CRT**: no curve arithmetic, no pairings, no trusted setup. The price is that it detects only **value-range violation** (not logical error), and that it requires the program to be free of order dependence.

**Corollary 7.4 (word-width demand of the reconstruction step)**. By §2.1(vi), the product of the four-prime pool, $\prod p_i\approx9.30\times10^{21}$, far exceeds $2^{63}-1\approx9.22\times10^{18}$. Hence the **reconstruction step** of CRT (Garner's algorithm or continued-fraction reconstruction) must be carried out on integers of width at least $73$ bits, whereas **arithmetic on the residue components themselves** (componentwise modular operations) is comfortably served by $64$ bits when $p_i<2^{21}$. This separation confines the cost of widening to $O(k)$ reconstruction operations, leaving the bulk $O(n)$ arithmetic untouched.

_An implementation erratum, since corrected_. An early implementation used 64-bit integers in the reconstruction step, producing silent overflow. The **classification** of that bug is worth recording: it belongs to the **value-range** category of Corollary 22.2, not the **order** category — it manifests as "computed wrong" rather than "cannot compute", an instance of the "high severity" row of that table. This confirms the priority order of §7.2 from the reverse direction: value-range failures are silent and hard to detect, hence must be guarded first. The three-channel certificate (Proposition 7.3) is precisely the run-time line of defence against this category.

**Exact rationals and the single entry point of rounding**. An exact real is a rational pair $(n,d)$, each component represented by a CRT residue vector, so **every internal operation is exact** (addition, subtraction, multiplication and division are all integer operations; division takes a modular inverse). Conversion to fixed point occurs **once**, when an external boundary is crossed (emitting a decimal string, or handing a value to an external floating-point consumer):
$$\text{internal: the exact }\frac{n}{d}\ \xrightarrow[\text{exactly once}]{\text{boundary}}\ \text{fixed point: }\left\lfloor \frac{n\cdot2^{b}}{d}\right\rceil$$
_Discipline_. A classical floating-point pipeline introduces and accumulates error at **every step** ($0.1\times10\neq1$); this pipeline rounds at **exactly one** place ($10\times\frac{1}{10}=1$ is exact throughout). This is the three-noes principle realized at the **numerical** layer rather than the arithmetic layer: "no floating point" is not a slogan about avoiding the `float` type but the architectural property that **error has a single entry point**. The choice of the width parameter $b$ and the implementation details are given in [Paper5].


## 8 The Residue Path Integral: Algebraization of Dynamics

**Definition 8.1**. A pole configuration $\kappa=(\{z_k,r_k\})$; the generating rule induces transition weights $w(\kappa_t\to\kappa_{t+1})\in GF(p)$; the evolution operator $U$ is an integer matrix on configuration space (unitary under the stratified semantics, Theorem 6).

**Proposition 8.2 (path sum = matrix power)**.
$$Z(A\to B;T)=(U^T)_{A\to B}=\sum_{\kappa_0=A}^{\kappa_T=B}\ \prod_{t=0}^{T-1}w(\kappa_t\to\kappa_{t+1})$$
Over a finite field the sum is finite, measure-theoretic ambiguity is zero, weights are exact integers, and the convolution structure is NTT-accelerable ($U$'s eigenbasis = NTT eigenroots).

**Isomorphism with the Cauchy contour theorem**:
$$\oint_\gamma\psi\,dz=2\pi i\sum_{z_k\in\text{int}(\gamma)}\text{Res}(\psi,z_k)$$
The contour can be deformed (homotopy-invariant), and path information collapses to the pole set — strictly isomorphic to the skeleton expansion of path integrals (only vertices enter the amplitude); gauge invariance = the finite-field prototype of path-deformation invariance (the Wilson-loop implementation, with Tr's cyclicity, has been verified).

**Numerical verification**: $U$ = π/4 rotation ($\cos=\sin=2040$): $U^2=[[0,-1],[1,0]]$, $U^4=-I$, $U^8=I$ (period 8), $UU^T=I$ (orthogonal = non-dissipative); amplitude iteration for 2/4/8 steps hits the $Z$ values exactly.

**Honest boundary**: what this paper gives is a discrete algebraic path sum, not a continuum functional-measure derivation; the continuum limit is T4.

### 8.5 NTT diagonalization of cyclic-structure operators: closed form of the path sum

Proposition 8.2's matrix power $U^T$ gains a closed form when the configuration has **one-dimensional translation-invariant structure** — precisely where the NTT shines.

**Lemma 8.3 (NTT diagonalization of circulant matrices)**. Let the poles be arranged on a one-dimensional ring with translation-invariant transition weights: $w(\kappa_j\to\kappa_k)=c_{(j-k)\bmod N}$. Then $U$ is circulant and
$$U=F^{-1}\,\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_{N-1})\,F,\qquad \lambda_m=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}c_k\,\omega^{mk},\qquad Z(A\to B;T)=\frac1N\sum_m\omega^{m(A-B)}\lambda_m^T$$
where $F$ is the NTT matrix (order-$N$ primitive root $\omega$: Fermat field $N\le2^{16}$; Mersenne torus $N\le2^{17}$, Theorem 6).

_Proof_. Diagonalization of circulants by the DFT is standard; over GF(p) the NTT replaces the DFT, and all eigenvalues $\lambda_m$ are exact integers with zero rounding. □

**Significance**: (i) the $T$-step path sum drops from repeated matrix powers to $O(N\log N+T)$ — the **NTT acceleration channel for path integrals**, applicable to phonon chains / spin chains / one-dimensional lattice field theories; (ii) general configurations (no translation invariance) still take the matrix-power route — **structure is a resource for acceleration**, homologous to the logic of §15's tensor networks. Note that the $2\times2$ rotation $[[c,-s],[s,c]]$ ($s\neq0$) verified in §8 is **not** circulant ($c_1$ cannot simultaneously equal $\mp s$), so the lemma does not apply — its period 8 is verified by direct matrix powers; structural assumptions must be declared explicitly, which corroborates §15.2's "tensor networks win by structure".

**Corollary 8.4 (NTT–Hebbian spectral learning — the $O(n\log n)$ realization of the $\Delta$ operator in the diagonal basis)**. The diagonalization of Lemma 8.3 applies equally to the $\Delta$ (Hebbian sculpting) operator. The Hebbian update defined in [Paper2] §2.5 is a pointwise operation $w_i\leftarrow w_i\oplus(\eta\odot x_i)$ on weight–signal pairs of length $n$, costing $O(n)$. When the update law is of **convolution type** (the weight update of a fully connected layer = the cyclic convolution of weights with signals), direct computation in the time domain costs $O(n^2)$; in the NTT diagonal basis it degenerates to a pointwise operation:
$$W'\ =\ \mathrm{INTT}\big(\ \mathrm{NTT}(W)\ \oplus\ (\eta\odot \mathrm{NTT}(X))\ \big)\ \in\ O(n\log n)$$
_Proof_. By the convolution theorem, cyclic convolution equals pointwise multiplication in the NTT basis. The NTT over GF$(p)$ is exact (a primitive root $\omega=g^{(p-1)/N}$ exists iff $N\mid p-1$, and the Fermat field supports $N\le2^{16}$), so this identity holds **exactly** in the finite field — formally identical to the convolution theorem for the complex FFT, but without floating-point error. □

_Meaning_. Three properties are obtained at once: ① **determinism** — no random initialization, no floating-point non-associativity; identical inputs give identical outputs and experiments are reproducible bit for bit; ② **hardware realizability** — the $\Delta$ operator already has a PE mapping (the HCU of [Paper2] §2.5, the PE_LAP unit); ③ **verifiability** — a training result is a sequence of GF$(p)$ elements, hence fingerprintable and bit-identically comparable across implementations.

_Honest boundary_. ① The spectral form is valid only for **cyclic-convolution-type** update laws; for pointwise updates ($O(n)$) the spectral route is slower, and the choice must follow the structure of the update law. ② "Learning" over a finite field is **not semantically equivalent** to learning over the reals: there is no vanishing/exploding gradient (values are bounded) but also no continuity, and the topology of the loss surface is entirely different — "convergence" over a finite field can only be defined as a **fixed point of strict equality** ($w_{\text{prev}}=w_{\text{curr}}$), not as an $\varepsilon$-neighbourhood. This is a genuine paradigm difference, neither a defect nor an advantage; it must be judged per application. ③ This paper does **not** claim that GF$(p)$ learning is superior to floating-point learning, only that it is strictly dominant along the two axes of "determinism" and "verifiability"; the inventory of algorithms implemented over the field is given in [Paper5].


### 8.6 The grand unification equation: Computation ≅ Res(P(z)/Q(z)) — temporal liusuization

This section gives the complete derivation and numerical anchors.

> **Grand unification equation**. $\text{Computation}\cong\text{Res}\big(P(z)/Q(z)\big)$ over GF(p): all computation (recurrences, data structures, algorithm execution, physical evolution) is isomorphic to residue extraction of a rational function — the coefficients of $P$ encode the initial data, the roots of $Q$ encode structure and dynamics, and extracting residues executes the computation.

**Theorem 12 (recurrence ↔ rational-function duality)**. The generating function $A(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n$ of a $k$-th order linear recurrence $a_n=c_1a_{n-1}+\cdots+c_ka_{n-k}$ is the rational function $P(z)/Q(z)$, with $Q(z)=1-c_1z-\cdots-c_kz^k$ and $P$ determined by the initial values; conversely, the Taylor coefficients of a rational function with denominator degree $\le k$ satisfy a $k$-th order linear recurrence.

_Proof_. A shift argument in the formal power series ring $GF(p)[[z]]$: the $z^n$ ($n\ge k$) coefficient of $Q(z)A(z)$ is $a_n-c_1a_{n-1}-\cdots-c_ka_{n-k}=0$, so the product truncates to a polynomial $P$ of degree $<k$ — a pure algebraic identity, holding verbatim over GF(p), with no limits and no analysis. Converse: after partial-fraction expansion, termwise expansion of $1/(1-\lambda z)$ yields the recurrence. □

**Theorem 13 (time poles and residue extraction = computation)**. Let the distinct roots of $Q$ be $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k$ (landing field decided by the §10 splitting criteria). Then:
(a) $A(z)=\sum_j \frac{r_j}{1-\lambda_j z}$ ($r_j$ = partial-fraction coefficients, exact over GF, computable by Berlekamp factorization);
(b) $a_n=\sum_j r_j\lambda_j^n$ — **the $n$-th term = residue extraction**; the characteristic roots $\lambda_j$ are the sequence's **time poles**: infinite history is fully locked by $k$ poles + residues (complete compression in the time dimension — the "universal recurrence");
(c) whether time poles land in the base field or an extension is decided by the **same** splitting-criterion table as spatial poles.

_Proof_. (a) Partial fractions hold exactly in the extension tower of $GF(p)$; (b) from $\frac1{1-\lambda z}=\sum_n\lambda^nz^n$ by coefficient comparison; (c) the QR criterion of §10 applied to the characteristic polynomial. □

**Worked example 1 (Fibonacci — time poles in an extension)**. Characteristic polynomial $x^2-x-1$, discriminant 5; 5 is an NR mod 65537 (verification item L4) ⟹ $\lambda_\pm=\frac{1\pm\sqrt5}{2}\in GF(p^2)=GF(p)[\sqrt5]$. The Binet form $F(n)=\frac{\lambda_+^n-\lambda_-^n}{\sqrt5}$ is exact within $GF(p^2)$. Verified numerically: $\lambda_+^2=\lambda_++1$ holds componentwise; $F(5)=5,\ F(10)=55,\ F(15)=610,\ F(20)=6765$ all exact — in three-way agreement with [Paper5]'s residue extraction and the naive recurrence.

**Worked example 2 (periodic sequence — time poles in the base field)**. The characteristic polynomial of $1,0,-1,0,\dots$ is $x^2+1$; $-1$ is a QR ($p\equiv1\bmod4$) ⟹ roots $\pm i=\pm256\in GF(p)$. $a_n=\frac{i^n+(-i)^n}{2}$: the first 8 terms $1,0,-1,0,1,0,-1,0$ and $n=8\to1$ (unit-root period closes) are all exact. **The same criterion table governs spatial poles (§10–§12) and time poles alike** — number-theoretic unity shows up again in the time dimension.

**Corollary 8.8 (SRRF inversion = Berlekamp–Massey)**. The solution to §5.5's honest negative result (27,550 seeds failing to converge by iteration): fixed points need not be approached by iteration — from a system's behavior sequence, invert the minimal recurrence by **Berlekamp–Massey** → factor by **Berlekamp** to obtain time poles → combine with spatial poles into the complete spectrum. **To know a system = to invert its spatial poles $\cup$ time poles** (P2's execution path; stage item T9).

## 12 The Fermat/Mersenne Dilemma Theorem and the Mersenne Partner Field

### 12.1 The dilemma theorem

The proofs of Theorems 5 and 6 rest on two standard lemmas, stated here first (both are direct consequences of quadratic reciprocity and Euler's criterion; proofs are given to keep this paper self-contained).

**Lemma 12.1 (splitting criterion)**. Let $p$ be an odd prime and $a\in GF(p)^\times$. Then
$$x^2-a\ \text{is reducible (splits) over}\ GF(p)\ \iff\ a\ \text{is a quadratic residue}\ \iff\ a^{(p-1)/2}\equiv 1\pmod p .$$
For the special case $a=-1$: $x^2+1$ splits over $GF(p)$ $\iff$ $p\equiv1\pmod4$.

_Proof_. If $a=b^2$ then $a^{(p-1)/2}=b^{p-1}\equiv1$ by Fermat's little theorem. Conversely, $GF(p)^\times$ is cyclic of order $p-1$; taking a primitive root $g$ and writing $a=g^t$, we have $a^{(p-1)/2}=g^{t(p-1)/2}\equiv1\iff(p-1)\mid t(p-1)/2\iff t$ is even $\iff a$ is a square. For $a=-1$, Euler's criterion gives $(-1/p)=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}$, and $(p-1)/2$ is even $\iff p\equiv1\pmod4$. □

**Lemma 12.2 (the Frobenius phase condition)**. Let $p$ be an odd prime, let $\sigma:x\mapsto x^p$ be the Frobenius automorphism, and let $\bar{\ }$ denote conjugation of the quadratic extension. For an element $i$ with $i^2=-1$:
$$\sigma(i)=i\cdot i^{p-1}=i\cdot(-1)^{(p-1)/2}=\begin{cases}\ \ i, & p\equiv1\pmod4\quad(\textbf{splitting})\\ -i=\bar i, & p\equiv3\pmod4\quad(\textbf{inert})\end{cases}$$
Three consequences follow, forming the algebraic basis of Theorems 5 and 6:

| | $p\equiv1\pmod4$ | $p\equiv3\pmod4$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| where $i$ lives | $i\in GF(p)$ ($x^2+1$ splits) | $i\notin GF(p)$ ($x^2+1$ irreducible) |
| Frobenius on $i$ | identity | conjugation $\bar{\ }$ |
| norm $N(i)=i^{p+1}=(-1)^{(p+1)/2}$ | $-1$ | $+1$ |
| the quadratic extension | $GF(p)[\sqrt{-1}]$ is **not a field** | $GF(p^2)=GF(p)[i]$ is a field |
| $x^2+y^2$ | isotropic (zero norm exists) | anisotropic (zero norm vanishes) |

_Proof_. $\sigma(i)=i^p=i\cdot i^{p-1}=i\cdot(i^2)^{(p-1)/2}=i\cdot(-1)^{(p-1)/2}$. For $p\equiv3\pmod4$, $\sigma(i)=-i$: Frobenius acts on $i$ by conjugation rather than identity, so $i\notin GF(p)$. For the norm, $N(i)=i\cdot\sigma(i)=i^{p+1}=(i^2)^{(p+1)/2}=(-1)^{(p+1)/2}$, which equals $1$ when $(p+1)/2$ is even ($p\equiv3$) and $-1$ when it is odd ($p\equiv1$; consistent with $i\in GF(p)$ giving $N(i)=i^2=-1$). **Corollary 22.5 (context-sensitive semantics — two inverses of the same literal)** [VERIFIED]. The literal $1/n$ occurs in **two different rings**:

- **division context** $a/n$: the inverse of $n$ is the **field inverse** $n^{-1}\bmod p$ (the multiplicative inverse in $\mathbb F_p$);
- **radical context** $a^{1/n}$: the inverse of $n$ is the **exponent-ring inverse** $n^{-1}\bmod(p-1)$ (the inverse in $\mathbb Z/(p-1)\mathbb Z$, since exponents take values in the exponent ring of the cyclic group).

For general $p$ the two **differ**. Measured over GF(65537):
$$\underbrace{8^{3^{-1}\bmod 65537}=8^{21846}=4}_{\text{field inverse (wrong semantics)}},\quad 4^3=64\neq8\ \color{red}{\times}\qquad\underbrace{8^{3^{-1}\bmod 65536}=8^{43691}=2}_{\text{exponent-ring inverse (correct semantics)}},\quad 2^3=8\ \color{green}{\checkmark}$$

_Significance_. This is **one syntax with two semantics** — not ambiguity but context determining the ambient ring. In classical language theory, "same syntax, semantics varying with context" is handled by overloading, an engineering convention; here the context sensitivity is **algebraic**: division and root extraction live in different group structures (the elements of $\mathbb F_p^\times$ versus the exponents of its endomorphism ring). An earlier treatment applied the field inverse uniformly, a **silent error** (producing the formally legal but wrong value $4$); this corollary is at once an erratum and a theorem. Back-substitution is the certificate: $2^3=8$ ✓, $2048^3=2$ ✓, $2^5=32$ ✓.

**Corollary 22.6 (the closed/split duality — a second proof of the existence of the prime pool)**. The closed condition $\gcd(n,p-1)=1$ and the split condition $n\mid(p-1)$ point in opposite directions:

- **Fermat pole**, $p-1=2^{16}$ (a pure 2-power): for every **odd** $n$, $\gcd(n,2^{16})=1$, so it is a **closed pole** — every odd radical has a unique, exact, error-free root; but it has no splitting capability for any $n\mid 2^{16}$ (apart from $n=2$).
- **Mersenne-type pole**, $p_2-1=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot17\cdot257$: rich in odd factors, so it is a **split pole** — $\zeta_3,\zeta_5,\zeta_{17},\zeta_{257}$ all lie in the base field and multi-root splitting is abundant; but for $n=3,5,17,257$ one has $\gcd>1$, so **none of them is closed**.

**One field can hardly be optimal at both**: closure requires $p-1$ to share no factor with $n$, splitting requires $p-1$ to be divisible by $n$. This pair of opposing requirements is exactly the **second proof of the necessity of a prime pool** (the first being the NTT/probability division of labour in Theorem 5, §2.1(ii)): since a single field cannot have both, a pool is mandatory — and the non-negative-asset disjunction in the admission criterion of Theorem 30 ($v_2\ge16$ **or** odd factors $\ge3$) is precisely the disjunction of these two capabilities. Three proofs (the division of labour between two dilemmas, root-of-unity complementarity, and the closed/split duality) converge on the same conclusion and corroborate one another.

**Relation to Theorem 28 (the taiji partition)**. The taiji partition asserts that "each field owns exactly the root the other lacks, and this division of labour is forced by $p\bmod3$ and $p\bmod4$". The closed/split duality is the projection of the taiji partition onto **radical theory**: the taiji partition says "who owns which root" (existence), while the closed/split duality says "having the root, how many roots can be extracted and how accurately" (solvability and precision). The depth-spectrum inversion at $k=9$ (Corollary 23.2: Fermat depth 2 < Mersenne depth 3) is likewise explained — "who owns the low-order root" and "who reaches the high-order root faster" are two independent dimensions, closed and split respectively.


### 12.9 The Lazy-Extension Theorem

Theorem 22 gives a static matching condition; this section lifts it to a dynamic evaluation strategy.

**Theorem 23 (lazy extension)**. There is a unique evaluation strategy: starting from $GF(p)$, stay while all current-layer characteristic polynomials are reducible; upon meeting an irreducible factor of degree $d$, automatically expand to $GF(p^d)$ (by Theorem 1, the pole already has a sub-field inside — drilling down is unfolding the nesting), then land back at reduced order. The strategy is (i) **correct**: results agree with pre-selecting the maximal extension; (ii) **optimal**: the depth reached is exactly the minimal order $k(P)$ demanded by Theorem 22.

**A formal system for field-order inference**. To turn Theorem 23 into an implementable algorithm, we give the following type system. Let the expression grammar be
$$e\ ::=\ x\ \mid\ c\ \mid\ e_1\,op\,e_2\ \mid\ \sqrt[n]{e}\ \mid\ \mathrm{domain}(e,p)$$
with judgments $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\ @\ k$, where $\tau$ is a type and $k$ a field order. Seven inference rules:

| Rule | Premise | Conclusion | Semantics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (Var) | $\Gamma(x)=\tau\ @\ k$ | $\Gamma\vdash x:\tau\ @\ k$ | a variable takes the environment's order |
| (Const) | $c\in GF(p)$ | $\Gamma\vdash c:GF(p)\ @\ 0$ | constants in the base field |
| (Op) | $\Gamma\vdash e_i:\tau_i\ @\ k_i$, $op$ well-defined on $F_{\mathrm{lcm}(k_1,k_2)}$ | $\Gamma\vdash e_1\,op\,e_2:\tau\ @\ \mathrm{lcm}(k_1,k_2)$ | operations take the least common extension field |
| (Root) | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\ @\ k$, $x^n-e$ irreducible over $F_k$ | $\Gamma\vdash\sqrt[n]{e}:F_{k+1}\ @\ k+1$ | **irreducibility raises the order** |
| (Embed) | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\ @\ k$, $k'\ge k$ | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\ @\ k'$ | embedding (raising) |
| (Project) | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\ @\ k$, $k'\le k$, projection exists | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\ @\ k'$ | projection (lowering) |
| (Auto) | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\ @\ k$, $k^*=\min\{m\le k\mid e\in F_m\}$ | $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\ @\ k^*$ | minimization fall-back |

An earlier draft's (Op) rule took $\max(k_1,k_2)$ — correct **only when the operands' orders form a containment chain ($k_1\mid k_2$)**, a leftover of "gear thinking": the compositum's degree is given by the divisibility lattice as $\mathrm{lcm}(k_1,k_2)$ (Theorem 23′(i)). Minimal counterexample (a group-theoretic refutation, no computation needed): take $a\in GF(3^2)\setminus GF(3)$ and $b\in GF(3^3)\setminus GF(3)$; if $a+b\in GF(3^3)$ then $a=(a+b)-b\in GF(3^3)$, contradicting $GF(3^2)\cap GF(3^3)=GF(3)$ — hence $\deg(a+b)=6=\mathrm{lcm}(2,3)$, and the max form drops the entire $GF(3^2)$ component. The cost of (Op)-lcm is one gcd (the Euclid machine, the fourth loading of Proposition 13.2). The other six rules are unaffected.

**The computable criterion of (Root)**. The key to adaptive field order is that irreducibility must be decidable. The classical criterion:
$$x^n-a\ \text{irreducible over}\ GF(q)\ \iff\ \forall\,\ell\mid n\ (\ell\ \text{prime}):\ a^{(q-1)/\ell}\neq1,\ \text{and if}\ 4\mid n\ \text{then}\ a^{(q-1)/4}\neq-1$$
This criterion (Lidl–Niederreiter) costs $O(\omega(n))$ modular exponentiations — affordable at compile time; the shorthand $a^{(q-1)/d}\neq1$ ($d=\gcd(n,q-1)$) is valid only for prime $n$. Hence both (Root) and (Auto) are mechanically executable, and the whole inference is isomorphic to Hindley–Milner type inference: field-order variables $\kappa$ correspond to type variables $\alpha$; the $\max$ of (Op) corresponds to unification; the minimization of (Auto) corresponds to principal-type resolution.

**The type-theoretic correspondence and its boundary**. The correspondence can be listed item by item: type variable $\alpha$ ↔ field-order variable $\kappa$; type unification ↔ merging of $k$; $\mathrm{let}$-polymorphism ↔ shell polymorphism $\forall k.\,\tau\,@\,k$; principal type ↔ minimal field order $k^*$; type error ↔ order mismatch (apparent unsolvability). In particular, the occurs check preventing infinite types in Hindley–Milner corresponds to the Zeno detection preventing infinite order-raising here — both are detection mechanisms for "the constraint system has no minimal fixed point". It must be said: this correspondence is currently **structural**; a strict categorical equivalence (if any) has not been established and is listed as an open problem.

_Proof idea_. By standard properties of lazy evaluation, combined with Theorems 12, 13 ($k$-th order recurrences correspond to degree-$k$ irreducible factors) and Theorem 1 (nesting = sub-fields). □

**Characteristic constraint**. The strategy can only change the extension degree within one characteristic: towers over $GF(p)$ are always $GF(p^k)$; the characteristic cannot be changed by extension — the Mersenne field cannot be generated from the Fermat field. Field choice is thus no longer a static territorial division but a tower automatically grown on each seed — the "add a prime" operation of Theorem 19 is replaced by dynamic unfolding. The Fermat and Mersenne seeds each grow a tower, and Theorem 20's 32768 twin relation gives the unique conversion between the two towers.

**Theorem 23′ (gear-free tower evaluation — adaptive field order and the dissolution of gears)**. In the algebraic closure $\bar{F}_p=\bigcup_k GF(p^k)$ (a direct limit):

(i) **The subfield lattice**. $GF(p^b)$ contains a subfield of $p^a$ elements iff $a\mid b$, and **at most one** — over a finite field, each degree has exactly one subfield. The tower's subfields thus form, under divisibility, the **divisibility lattice**: join (compositum) $GF(p^a)\vee GF(p^b)=GF(p^{\mathrm{lcm}(a,b)})$; meet (intersection) $GF(p^{\gcd(a,b)})$.

(ii) **Degree is intrinsic to values**. Every element $x$ carries an intrinsic degree $d(x)=[GF(p)(x):GF(p)]$ (the degree of its minimal polynomial). For any rational expression $e(x_1,\dots,x_n)$: $d(e)\mid\mathrm{lcm}_i\,d(x_i)$, and the bound is attained for suitable inputs (compositum theory) — the field order of an expression is a **derived quantity of its operands' degrees**, not a machine state to be stored.

(iii) **Gear-free evaluation and equivalence with Theorem 23**. Per-value degree-tagged evaluation — each value stored in its own minimal field with its degree attached; before each operation, operands are lifted through the unique subfield embeddings to the lcm-degree field; after it, a degree drop is detected by minimal-polynomial computation (**free downshift**, costing one polynomial gcd) — yields results **bit-identical** to Theorem 23's global lazy extension; Theorem 23 is this mechanism's **coarsening**, in which an entire evaluation phase shares one running maximal degree.

(iv) **Gears do not exist in characteristic $p$**. The notion of "shifting" reifies a **choice of representation**; by (i), each degree's placement in the tower is unique — there is no choice data anywhere. The residual $GF(p^d)$-automorphisms (the Frobenius group, $d$ of them) do not move the subfield's position: they are the level's internal symmetries already studied in Theorem 27's lineage, not choice data. **The tower has no gears because the tower has no choices.** (Contrast: extensions of $\mathbb Q$ have multiple embeddings — there a choice of representation is real data; gear-freeness is a distinctive gift of characteristic $p$.)

_Proof_. (i), (ii), (iv) are standard finite-field structure (uniqueness of subfields and compositum theory); (iii): evaluation semantics depend only on the mathematical value in the algebraic closure, so the two schedules differ purely at the representation layer and agree bit for bit; the coarsening relation follows directly from the definition of the running maximal degree. □

_Meaning and engineering position_. Theorem 23′ pushes the type-system reading of §12.9 to its conclusion: **the field order passes from "a globally inferred compile-time quantity" to "an intrinsic type of each value"** — the per-value instantiation of shell polymorphism $\forall k.\tau@k$ (Series V §13); the (Auto) rule's minimalization fall-back becomes a per-value minimal-degree fall-back. It also explains why "multi-structure coexistence fields" of the GF(41) type ($i$ and $\zeta_5$ coexisting, $\zeta_3$ absent — the factor signature $40=2^3\cdot5$) need not become a new "gear": once the field order is a per-value property, any prime's factor signature takes effect automatically, and **the gear table degenerates into the divisibility lattice**. Honest boundary: ① going gear-free **decentralizes cost without eliminating it** — per-value representation requires storing minimal polynomials and post-operation gcd checks; for workloads uniformly needing one fixed level throughout, Theorem 23's global gear amortizes better; the engineering recommendation is gear-free at the semantic layer (the type system) and a workload-dependent choice at the execution layer. ② This theorem dissolves only the **vertical axis** (degree choice within one characteristic); the **horizontal axis** (which prime $p$ to enter) remains governed by Corollary 22.2's range constraint and §14.3's entry lower bound — a genuine architectural decision that cannot be automated away. ③ The RN bridge (Corollary 20.1) is orthogonal to this theorem: the former answers "which prime to enter"; the latter, "how deep to go in the tower once entered".


**Corollary (the $k$-depth complexity hierarchy)**. The maximal nesting depth constitutes a third complexity axis orthogonal to time and space; "solvable at depth $\le k$" gives a strict hierarchy ($x^{k+1}-a$ irreducible separates adjacent levels). Combined with Theorem 14's parallel structure, different parts of a computation may sit at different extension levels — the computation as a whole is no longer a single global field but a $k$-order mosaic (categorically, a section of the tower's fibration).

**Open problem (shifting cost)**. The cost of raising $GF(p)$ to $GF(p^d)$ is so far only qualitative; a quantitative cost function $\mathrm{cost}(k\to k')$ is lacking, as is a formalization of "how to partition so as to minimize extension count". Giving this function is a necessary condition for upgrading the intuition above into a quantitative theory.


**Corollary 23.1 (the degree–order duality)**. For $\lambda\in\bar F_p^\times$ of multiplicative order $n=\mathrm{ord}(\lambda)$, the intrinsic degree of $\lambda$ is
$$\deg(\lambda)=\mathrm{ord}_n(p)$$
— **the degree is the orbit length of Frobenius on the element's order**: the multiplicative order of $p$ modulo $n$. This is the exact dual of the period laws of Theorems 25/26: the period reads **the element's order** $\mathrm{ord}(\lambda)$, the degree reads **Frobenius's order on that element** $\mathrm{ord}_{\mathrm{ord}(\lambda)}(p)$. Machine-verified instances: $i=65281$ (order 4; $\mathrm{ord}_4(65537)=1$ ⟹ degree 1); $\zeta_3=109165$ (order 3; $\mathrm{ord}_3(131071)=1$ ⟹ degree 1); the Fibonacci time pole $\lambda_+$ (order dividing $2(p{+}1)$ with a part not dividing $p{-}1$ ⟹ $\mathrm{ord}_n(65537)=2$ ⟹ degree 2); and the **divisibility-lattice join instance** (in $GF(41^6)$, over the irreducible $t^6{+}7t^5{+}t^4{+}35t^3{+}8t^2{+}30t{+}23$): $\deg(\alpha)=2$, $\deg(\beta)=3$, $\deg(\alpha\beta)=6=\mathrm{lcm}(2,3)$, verified term by term.

**Corollary 23.2 (the cyclotomic depth law — the vertical coordinate of the tower)**. The minimal depth at which $\zeta_k$ appears in the tower over $GF(p)$ is
$$d_{\min}(k)=\mathrm{ord}_k(p)$$
($k\mid p^d-1\iff \mathrm{ord}_k(p)\mid d$). Hence what remains after the gear table dissolves is not "no table" but a **two-coordinate table** — the horizontal coordinate $p_{\min}(k)$ (the tower-entry prime of Series V §14.3) and the vertical coordinate $d_{\min}(k)$ (this corollary) jointly locate any root-of-unity demand exactly. The dual-tower depth table (all machine-verified):

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 5 | 4 | 1 | Theorems 21/29, $\chi_5$ on the Mersenne base field |

Three quantitative consequences: ① **the taiji partition quantified** — $\zeta_{17}=32768$ sits at depth 1 in the Mersenne tower and depth 8 in the Fermat tower, upgrading Theorem 28's "complementarity" from qualitative to an 8:1 depth ratio; ② **a depth-spectrum inversion** — the Mersenne field, though holding $\zeta_3$ in its base field (which the Fermat field lacks), reaches $\zeta_9$ at depth 3 while the Fermat tower reaches it at depth 2 — "who owns the low-order root" and "who reaches the high-order root faster" are independent questions; ③ **same-depth pairs** — $k=7,19,31$ have identical depths in both towers ($6,6$/$9,9$/$30,30$), a direct consequence of the siting table's cases $p_1\equiv p_2\pmod k$.

**Erratum on the GF(41) instance (a correction to the earlier engineering draft)**. Earlier program documents described GF(41)'s multi-structure coexistence as "$\deg(i)=2$, $\deg(\zeta_5)=4$, $\mathrm{lcm}=4$" — **this is a computational error**. Measured: $41\equiv1\pmod4$ so $i=9\in GF(41)$ (degree 1), and $5\mid40$ so $\zeta_5=10\in GF(41)$ (degree 1), with $i+\zeta_5=19$ again of degree 1 — **multi-structure coexistence is a base-field phenomenon of the factor signature $p-1=40=2^3\cdot5$, involving no degree mixing at all**. A genuine divisibility-lattice join requires a real extension: the $GF(41^6)$ verification of Corollary 23.1 ($2\times3\to6$). The erratum itself demonstrates the error-detection power of the gear-free framework: once degree is a per-value property, any degree-mixing written under "gear intuition" is instantly refuted by a minimal-polynomial computation.

**Corollary 23.3 (the lattice laws of the depth table — the full-depth spectrum and the worst-case depth bound)**. The depth table itself is organized by the divisibility lattice:

(i) **Coprime composition law**: for $\gcd(m,n)=1$, $\mathrm{ord}_{mn}(p)=\mathrm{lcm}(\mathrm{ord}_m(p),\mathrm{ord}_n(p))$ (16 coprime compositions verified across both towers; $k=15{=}3\cdot5$: depth 4 = lcm(2,4); $k=21$: 6; $k=45{=}9\cdot5$: 4). Hence the depth table needs only its **prime-power rows** — the depth of any composite is computable by lcm, and the lookup cost drops to $O(\omega(k))$ gcds.

(ii) **The full-depth spectrum**: for a prime $q$, $d_{\min}(q)=q-1$ iff $p$ is a primitive root mod $q$. Measured ($q\le43$): the Fermat tower's $65537$ is a primitive root mod $\{3,5,7,23,29,31,41,43\}$ (8 full-depth points); the Mersenne tower's $131071$ is a primitive root mod $\{7,11,23,31,37,41\}$; and **$41$ is a full-depth point in both towers** — adding a further layer of depth signature to the $p=41$ triple resonance of Corollary 29.1 ($d_{\min}(8)=d_{\min}(5)=1$ while $d_{\min}(3)=2$: $\zeta_8$ and $\zeta_5$ coexist in the base field while $\zeta_3$ needs one level — the depth signature of $40=2^3\cdot5$).

(iii) **The Artin restatement (a change of language, not progress)**: the tower's depth spectrum contains infinitely many full-depth primes $\iff$ Artin's primitive-root conjecture. The "tower top" of Theorem 19(iv) thus acquires its precise gear-free form: that the tower has infinitely many full-height lattice points is a conjecture; that every point's depth is computable is a theorem.

(iv) **Worst-case depth bound**. The worst-case cost of gear-free navigation is **linear** and trivially tight:

- **Upper bound (unconditional)**. By Euler's theorem $\mathrm{ord}_k(p)\mid\varphi(k)$ and $\varphi(k)<k\le x$, hence
$$\max_{\substack{k\le x\\ (k,p)=1}}\mathrm{ord}_k(p)\ \le\ x-1 .$$

- **Lower bound (unconditional, constructive)**. By the LTE formula, for $m>v_2(p^2-1)$,
$$\mathrm{ord}_{2^m}(p)=2^{\,m-v_2(p^2-1)+1}$$
(verified term by term for $p=65537,131071,181,3,5$). Taking $m=\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor$,
$$\max_{k\le x}\mathrm{ord}_k(p)\ \ge\ 2^{\,\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor-v_2(p^2-1)+1}\ \ge\ \frac{x}{2^{\,v_2(p^2-1)}} .$$

Hence $\max_{k\le x}\mathrm{ord}_k(p)=\Theta_p(x)$, with the implicit constant depending only on $p$.

**Measured** ($p=65537$): for $x=100/500/2000/10000$ the maxima are $88/498/1996/9966$, attained at $k=89/499/1997/9967$ (all prime), i.e. at $q^\ast-1$ where $q^\ast$ is the largest prime $\le x$ of which $65537$ is a primitive root — the upper bound $x-1$ is approached asymptotically at every measured point.

_An easily committed citation error_. One must **not** invoke here the asymptotics $\log g(n)\sim\sqrt{n\log n}$ of the Landau function
$$g(n)=\max\Big\{\mathrm{lcm}(n_1,\dots,n_r)\ :\ \textstyle\sum_i n_i=n\Big\}$$
(Landau 1903; refined by Nicolas 1988): the argument of $g$ is a **sum of parts** (the degree of a permutation) whereas the bounding variable here is a **bound on the modulus**; and $g$'s superlinear growth comes from **partition-type** composition ($\sum n_i\mapsto\mathrm{lcm}$, where the partition constraint lets $\mathrm{lcm}$ far exceed $\sum n_i$), whereas depth composition in this paper is **product-type** ($mn\mapsto\mathrm{lcm}$, item (i), with $\mathrm{ord}_k(p)$ always bounded by $k$). The two differ enormously: at $x=10^4$, $\Theta_p(x)$ gives $9966$ while $\exp\sqrt{x\log x}$ gives about $6\times10^{131}$. Every depth composition in this paper follows the product-type path.

_Meaning of the bound_. The vertical axis has neither gears nor depth explosion — the worst-case depth does not exceed the modulus itself, and the bound is trivially tight (attained at the prime points above).

**Corollary 23.4 (eliminability of the domain marker — self-describing encoding)**. Theorem 23′(ii) asserts that the field order $d(x)$ is an intrinsic property of the value rather than a machine state. One direct consequence is that **the domain marker can be eliminated**: if the field's identity is encoded in the residue field of the pole itself (taking $r=p$, the prime), then the pole states which field it lives in — no index, no symbol table, no accompanying metadata is needed.

_Proof_. By the uniqueness of subfields (Theorem 23′(i)), a field is determined by its characteristic $p$ and degree $d$; when the marker is $p$ itself, field information becomes a **sub-structure** of the pole rather than an external annotation. The converse holds as well: given the state of the domain stack, the emission sequence of domain-marker poles can be replayed, so marker and pole **mutually determine one another** — they are two projections of one piece of information, and by the reduction criterion of series V they constitute a redundant representation. □

_Significance_. Corollary 23.4 is the cashing-in of the **negative form** of Theorem 23′: precisely because the field order is intrinsic (not choosable, not externally assignable), it can be inlined as part of the value without needing to be synchronized against any external table. This contrasts with classical executable formats (ELF/PE/WASM), which rely on symbol tables or index spaces to resolve cross-module references — there the metadata is separated from the code, and synchronization is the dominant source of link errors. The **representation layer** (serialization protocol, cross-language reader, comparison with the "accompanying schema" route of DWARF/JSON-Schema) is developed in [Paper5].

_Boundary_. Self-description holds only for **prime fields** (the residue slot accommodates $p$). For an extension $GF(p^d)$ an extra slot is needed for $d$; after degenerating to a tuple, self-description is partly retained ($p$ self-describes, $d$ must be stipulated).


### 12.10 The Strict k-Depth Hierarchy and Field-Order Inference

This section presents Theorem 23's formal system and three verified conclusions.

**Theorem 24 (strict k-depth hierarchy)**. Define towers $F_{k+1}=F_k[x]/(x^{k+1}-a_k)$ (when $x^{k+1}-a_k$ is irreducible over $F_k$; irreducibility is computably decidable: $x^n-a$ is irreducible over $GF(q)$ iff for every prime factor $\ell$ of $n$, $a^{(q-1)/\ell}\neq1$ (and if $4\mid n$, $a^{(q-1)/4}\neq-1$)). Then "solvable at depth $\le k$" is strictly contained in "solvable at depth $\le k{+}1$", and $k$ is **orthogonal** to classical time and space complexity — it measures a problem's algebraic-geometric position, not resource consumption (there exist problems with time and space $O(1)$ requiring $k=5$).

_Proof idea_. Irreducibility entails that no $F_k$-algebra homomorphism $F_{k+1}\to F_k$ exists; orthogonality follows by construction, as $k$ enters neither step nor space counts. Full formalization is T14.

**Field-order inference (the algorithmic form of Theorem 23)**: the seven Hindley–Milner-style rules of §12.9 (constant layer 0; operations take the operand maximum; irreducible roots trigger raising; embed; project; auto takes the minimal $k^*$). The pipeline has three stages: constraint collection ($\sqrt[n]{a}$ contributes $n\mid p-1$), constraint solving ($\text{lcm}\mid p-1$; the minimal prime can be auto-discovered — e.g., $\text{lcm}(5,4)=20$ gives $p=41$, precisely the Ramsey-critical prime, whose structural explanation is the Sachs double-prohibition analysis below), and partitioning (different sub-expressions routed to different primes, glued by CRT, subject to Corollary 22.2's dual constraint). Here "shell polymorphism" $\forall k.\,\tau@k$ is a new programming-language concept, and type inference can be viewed as field-order inference at $k=0$.

**A shifting-cost model**. The inference above answers "can we shift" but not "is it worth shifting". A computable cost model: let $\alpha$ be the per-order re-encoding cost and $\beta$ the fixed cross-field gluing cost; then
$$\mathrm{cost}(k)\approx k\log_2 p,\qquad \mathrm{cost}(k\to k')\approx |k-k'|\log_2 p\cdot\alpha\ +\ \delta_{k\neq k'}\cdot\beta$$
($\delta$ the Kronecker symbol; gluing cost counted only when the field changes). The (Auto) rule's minimization thus gains a definite objective: among all feasible orders $m\le k$, take the cost-minimal one. Decision rule: raise iff the "unsolvability penalty" avoided exceeds $\mathrm{cost}(k\to k')$; since that penalty is effectively infinite, raising is mandatory on irreducibility, and among multiple feasible fields the cheapest is chosen.

_Honest boundary_. The model is first-order; the concrete values of $\alpha,\beta$ are implementation-dependent (e.g., whether re-encoding can be made lazy, whether gluing can be merged with other conversions) and lack computed calibration. Under Theorem 23′'s per-value reading, this model measures the coarsened gear's cost; per-value scheduling decentralizes the cost into per-value re-encodings (the model's structure is unchanged, with the $\alpha$ term counted per value).

**Three verified conclusions**: ① the earlier claim (recorded in Collision III) that the Paley(41) graph contains no $K_5$ was **corrected on review** — computation shows 205 $K_5$s exist (the graph is self-complementary, so independent 5-sets are equally many); the mechanism of the 41-vertex construction's failure is clique growth. ② the association of $p=41$ with Ramsey criticality is confirmed. ③ the $7+14+15+6$ block structure of Exoo-42 is **so far only a hypothesis** — it still requires a block-rank test on the true adjacency matrix ($A=A_2\oplus A_3\oplus A_5\oplus A_7+A_{\text{cross}}$, each block's rank over $GF(p)$ at most $p-1$). This is the precise form of P6②, pending execution.

**The structural explanation of $p=41$ (new result)**. Previously only a "numerical resonance of $p=41$ with Ramsey criticality" was recorded; the structural reason is as follows. ## 13 The Born Rational-Reconstruction Theorem

### 13.1 Dyadic rationality of probabilities

**Lemma 13.1**. For an ideal quantum circuit (Clifford+T, computational-basis input and measurement), every Born probability is rational with a power-of-two denominator: $P=n/2^m$.

_Proof_. The gate matrix entries of Clifford+T lie in the ring $\mathbb Z[\frac1{\sqrt2},i]$; for computational-basis measurement, in $P=|\langle x|\psi\rangle|^2$ the $\sqrt2$ factors cancel in pairs (modulus-squaring kills odd powers of $i$ and $\sqrt2$ — $|\frac{a+b\sqrt2}{2^k}|^2=\frac{a^2+2b^2}{2^{2k+1}}$), so $P\in\mathbb Z[\frac12]$. Induction on circuit depth: each added gate multiplies the denominator by at most 4. □

### 13.2 Single-prime reconstruction and ambiguity analysis

**Theorem 7 (single-prime rational reconstruction)**. If $x\equiv n/d\pmod p$ ($d\not\equiv0$), $|n|\le B$, $0<d\le B$, and $2B^2<p$, then $(n,d)$ is uniquely determined by $x$.

_Proof_ (uniqueness). If $n/d\equiv n'/d'$, then $nd'\equiv n'd\pmod p$ while $|nd'-n'd|\le|n|d'+|n'|d\le2B^2<p$; hence the integer equality $nd'=n'd$, i.e., $\frac nd=\frac{n'}{d'}$. Constructively: the extended Euclid algorithm on $(x,p)$, taking the first remainder within bounds. □

**Machine demonstration of in-bound ambiguity**: $x=643\cdot1024^{-1}\equiv24385$. The true denominator $1024>181=\lfloor\sqrt{p/2}\rfloor$ — out of bounds; but an **in-bounds pseudo-solution exists**: $d=43\le181$, $n=x\cdot43\equiv65500$ ($|n|=37\le181$), i.e., $-\frac{37}{43}\equiv\frac{643}{1024}\pmod p$. Single-prime reconstruction returns the wrong answer here — out of bounds means ambiguity; this is not an implementation defect but the nature of modular arithmetic, and the only remedy is widening the bound (Theorem 8).

**In-bounds demonstration**: the Bell probability $2040^2=32769\equiv\frac12$ ($d=2\le181$) — unique correct reconstruction ✓ (verification item B1).

### 13.3 CRT widening

**Theorem 8 (CRT rational-reconstruction widening)**. For a prime set $\{p_i\}_{i=1}^k$ with $P=\prod p_i$: the denominator bound rises to $B_k\approx\sqrt{P/2}$, i.e., $k$ primes of 16–17 bits give $2^{\Theta(k)}$.

_Proof_. As Theorem 7 with the modulus replaced by $P$; the residue vector is combined by CRT into the class mod $P$. □

### 17.1 Engineering implementations of determinism

This section explains two implementation trade-offs at the computational layer, both serving the core goal of "bit-wise recomputability".

**Overflow avoidance in finite-field multiplication**. Under 32-bit integer semantics, $a\cdot b$ in $GF(65537)$ can reach about $2^{32}$ — direct multiplication overflows. The implementation does not take the classic "make a big number, then reduce" path but computes by doubling (Russian-peasant multiplication): each round performs only $r+x$ and $x+x$ (both $<131072<2^{31}$) with immediate reduction, keeping all intermediates strictly below $2^{17}$. The cost: multiplication becomes $O(\log b)$ additions; the gain: **zero overflow risk and identical behavior across the Rust, WASM, and VM backends**.

It should be noted that both $65537=2^{16}+1$ and $131071=2^{17}-1$ support shift-and-mask fast reduction (Fermat and Mersenne reduction respectively); the current implementation forgoes this optimization for cross-backend consistency — a clear performance opportunity, not a technique already in use.

**Interface-level determinism**. In the base arithmetic library, the random-number interface is implemented as a constant zero. This design makes "determinism" independent of test coverage or call discipline — it is guaranteed by the interface itself: no caller can introduce randomness through it. This closes the loop at the interface layer with §13.6's deterministic collapse and the three-nothing principle.
